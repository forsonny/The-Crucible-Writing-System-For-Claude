# Romantasy Question Overrides

Genre-specific answer options for the Crucible Planner questionnaire. These replace or supplement the base options when the Romantasy genre is selected.

When presenting questions to the user, use these romantasy-flavored options instead of the generic defaults. The question text, numbering, and state keys remain the same -- only the answer options change.

---

## Document 1: Crucible Thesis

### Q1.1 — The Burden

**What form does the external burden take?**

A) **A magical bargain or contract** -- A binding agreement (with a fae lord, a dark entity, a political power) that ties the protagonist to the love interest or to a course of action that brings them into repeated contact. The terms are precise and the consequences for breaking them are severe.

B) **A curse that requires genuine love to break** -- Not "true love's kiss" as a simple unlock, but a curse whose breaking demands real emotional vulnerability, trust, and choice. The curse worsens as the story progresses, creating urgency.

C) **A political marriage that must become real** -- An arranged union for political, magical, or strategic reasons. The burden is making it work -- not just performing the role, but navigating the genuine feelings that develop within an arrangement neither chose.

D) **A quest that requires the bonded pair** -- A mission that can only be completed by two people working together, specifically these two people. Their combined powers, bloodlines, or positions are the only key. They must learn to trust each other to survive.

E) **Protecting someone whose safety is tied to the love interest** -- A person the protagonist must keep alive or free, whose fate is entangled with the love interest's choices, loyalties, or power. The love interest is both the path to saving this person and the complication.

F) **Other** (describe)

### Q1.2 — The Fire

**What is the nature of the protagonist's Fire?**

A) **A mating bond or soul bond that grows overwhelming** -- A magical connection that intensifies with proximity, emotional honesty, or physical contact. It cannot be suppressed indefinitely. Fighting it causes pain; surrendering to it means vulnerability. The bond reveals truths the protagonist is not ready to face.

B) **Magical power that amplifies through emotional connection** -- The protagonist's magic grows stronger when they are emotionally open, particularly with the love interest. Strong feelings (desire, love, anger, grief) cause power surges that are both useful and dangerous. Emotional walls limit their power; vulnerability unleashes it.

C) **A curse tied to desire or love** -- The protagonist carries a curse that activates or worsens through romantic/sexual desire, love, or emotional attachment. Falling in love is literally dangerous. The curse may transform them, drain their life, harm those they care about, or attract enemies.

D) **Power that only manifests through physical contact with the love interest** -- Alone, the protagonist has limited or no magical ability. Through touch (or proximity, or emotional resonance) with the love interest, power awakens. This creates a dependency that both resent and need, and every use of power requires intimacy.

E) **A forbidden magical heritage that the love interest mirrors** -- The protagonist carries blood, power, or a legacy that is forbidden, hunted, or feared. The love interest carries the same heritage or its complement. Together they represent something the world tried to destroy. Their connection is both beautiful and politically catastrophic.

F) **Emotional magic that the protagonist cannot fully control** -- Magic fueled by the protagonist's emotional state. Joy creates light, rage creates fire, grief creates storms. Love -- the most powerful emotion -- creates the most dangerous and uncontrollable magic. Falling in love means becoming a magical wildcard.

G) **Other** (describe)

### Q1.3 — Core Constellation Bond

**Which bond will be the protagonist's anchor -- the one that CANNOT break?**

A) **Enemies forced into alliance** -- They despise each other, or they should. Opposing sides, old grudges, genuine harm done. But circumstances force them to work together, and what they discover is that the person they hated is the person they understand most deeply. The enmity was the wall; behind it is recognition.

B) **Political marriage becoming real** -- What began as duty, strategy, or survival becomes the truest thing in either of their lives. The shift from performing a role to living a truth is gradual, terrifying, and eventually undeniable.

C) **Fated mates resisting the bond** -- The universe says they belong together. They disagree. One or both fight the bond -- out of pride, fear, past trauma, or the legitimate desire to choose their own path. The tension between fate and choice IS the love story.

D) **Mentor and student crossing forbidden lines** -- One holds power, knowledge, or authority over the other. The attraction is real but the ethics are complicated. For this to work, the power imbalance must be addressed directly and resolved before the relationship can become equal.

E) **Captor and captive developing genuine feelings** -- One holds the other against their will. This trope requires extreme care: the captive must maintain agency, the captor must be shown as genuinely wrong in their captivity, and the shift to equality must be earned through real change, not Stockholm syndrome.

F) **Childhood friends reunited as changed people** -- They knew each other before. Now they are different -- harder, more powerful, carrying wounds the other did not witness. The tension between who they were and who they have become drives the romantic rediscovery.

### Q1.7 — The Surrender

**At the Apex, what essential thing must the protagonist willingly surrender?**

A) **Their emotional walls and vulnerability** -- The protagonist has survived by keeping people at arm's length. Surrendering means opening fully -- not just to the love interest, but to the possibility of loss, grief, and need. They must choose to be breakable in order to be whole.

B) **Acceptance of the bond they have been fighting** -- They have resisted, denied, rationalized, and run from the connection. The surrender is stopping the fight. Not because they are forced, but because they finally understand that fighting the bond is fighting themselves.

C) **Love over duty, power, or independence** -- Everything they have built, earned, or inherited stands on one side. The person they love stands on the other. The surrender is choosing the person over the position, knowing what it will cost.

D) **Trusting the love interest with their deepest wound** -- Not just telling them about the wound, but giving them access to the vulnerable place where it lives. Trusting that the love interest will not use it as a weapon. This is the ultimate intimacy -- more naked than any physical scene.

E) **The person they were before love** -- The version of themselves that was safe, controlled, and alone. Surrendering it means accepting that love has changed them permanently, and they cannot go back to who they were, and they do not want to.

F) **Their power, given freely for the love interest's sake** -- Not a sacrifice demanded by circumstance, but a choice. They give up the Fire -- the magic, the bond-power, the thing that makes them extraordinary -- because the love interest's life, freedom, or happiness requires it. And they would make the same choice again.

## Document 4: Constellation Strand Map

### Q4.3 — Betrayal Source

**Which relationship strains to breaking?**

A) **Misunderstanding about the love interest's motives** -- Evidence mounts that the love interest is using the protagonist, playing a deeper game, or has been lying about their feelings. The misunderstanding is rooted in genuine information that has been misinterpreted, not lazy miscommunication.

B) **Love interest's secret revealed** -- The love interest has been hiding something significant: their true identity, a past crime, an ongoing deception, a conflicting loyalty. The reveal is devastating not because of the secret itself but because it proves they did not trust the protagonist enough to share it.

C) **Third-party interference from court or family** -- Someone with power and motive deliberately works to separate the couple. A scheming courtier, a disapproving family, a political faction that needs the match to fail. The interference is effective because it exploits real insecurities.

D) **Duty versus desire forcing a choice** -- An irreconcilable conflict between what the protagonist owes (their people, their oath, their mission) and what they want (the love interest). For a time, it appears they cannot have both, and they must choose.

E) **Past trauma triggering a push-away** -- The relationship reaches a depth that activates old wounds. The protagonist (or love interest) sabotages the relationship not because they do not care, but because caring this much is terrifying. They push the other person away to protect themselves.

F) **Other** (describe)

## Document 6: Dark Mirror Profile

### Q6.4 — Compelling Offer

**At Beat 26, what alternative will the antagonist propose?**

A) **Power without the vulnerability of love** -- The Dark Mirror offers strength, safety, and control. No one will ever hurt the protagonist again. The price: close the heart. Seal the bond. Choose invulnerability over connection. The offer is tempting because vulnerability has already cost them so much.

B) **Safety without risking the heart** -- A life where nothing is at stake emotionally. No bond that could be broken. No person whose loss could shatter them. The Dark Mirror models this life -- powerful, secure, and hollow. The protagonist can see how appealing it would be to stop feeling so much.

C) **A match that is politically perfect but emotionally hollow** -- An alliance, a marriage, a partnership that makes strategic sense. The "right" choice by every measurable standard except the one that matters. The Dark Mirror argues that love is a luxury the world cannot afford.

D) **The love interest offered as a sacrifice for the greater good** -- The antagonist frames it as a trade: give up this one person and save thousands. The math is compelling. The morality is clear. And the protagonist must decide whether love is selfish or whether sacrificing love would destroy the thing worth saving.

E) **A version of love that is really control** -- The Dark Mirror offers their own twisted version of devotion: "I will never leave you, never betray you, never let anyone hurt you." It sounds like love. It is a cage. The protagonist must recognize the difference between being protected and being possessed.

F) **Other** (describe)

## Document 7: Constellation Bible

### Q7.1 — Protagonist Profile: The Wound

**What haunts the protagonist from before the story?**

A) **Betrayed by a previous love** -- Someone they loved used that love against them. The betrayal taught them that opening their heart is an invitation to be destroyed. They carry this lesson like armor, and it takes the entire story to convince them it was the wrong lesson.

B) **A belief that they are unworthy of love** -- Not just insecurity but a deep conviction. Maybe they were told they were unlovable. Maybe the people who should have loved them did not. Maybe they did something they believe makes them undeserving. This wound makes every gesture of affection feel like a trick.

C) **Love used as a weapon against them** -- Someone -- a parent, a mentor, a former partner -- wielded love as a tool of manipulation. "If you loved me, you would..." became the pattern. Now genuine love looks identical to manipulation, and the protagonist cannot tell the difference.

D) **Loss of someone they loved deeply** -- Grief as a wound. The protagonist loved once, fully, and lost that person. The prospect of loving again means accepting the possibility of that devastation again. They have chosen numbness over the risk.

E) **Never been loved or chosen** -- The absence wound. They have never been someone's first choice, never been fought for, never been the person someone stayed for. They do not know what love looks like from the receiving end, which means they do not recognize it when it arrives.

F) **Other** (describe)

## Document 9: World Forge

### Q9.1 — World's Wound

**What cosmic problem does the Quest address?**

A) **An ancient curse requiring true love to break** -- Not a fairy tale cliche but a world-scale enchantment. The land, the people, the magic itself is cursed, and the curse was designed to be unbreakable because its creators believed true love was impossible. The protagonist and love interest must prove them wrong.

B) **A magical imbalance that bonded pairs could heal** -- The world's magic is fracturing, dying, or corrupting. The solution requires paired magic -- two people bound by genuine connection channeling power together. But bonded pairs have become extinct, forbidden, or hunted.

C) **A war between courts or kingdoms that a union could end** -- Two powers locked in a conflict that has lasted generations. A union between their people -- a genuine one, not a political performance -- could forge peace. But both sides would rather keep fighting than see their enemy humanized by love.

D) **Dying magic that love-bonds could restore** -- Magic is fading from the world. The old sources are exhausted. But magic was always fueled by connection, by emotion, by the bonds between people. In a world that has grown cold and isolated, love is literally the cure -- and no one believes in it anymore.

E) **A prophecy about two souls ending or saving the world** -- Ancient texts speak of two who will come together and either save or destroy everything. The protagonist and love interest fit the prophecy. The question is which outcome their union will produce -- and whether the prophecy is a prediction or a warning.

F) **Other** (describe)

### Q9.2 — Power Source

**What is the source of power in this world?**

A) **Emotion-fueled magic** -- Magic in this world is powered by feeling. Joy, rage, grief, desire, love -- each emotion produces different magical effects. Love is the most powerful and most dangerous fuel. This means the protagonist's growing feelings for the love interest are literally changing their magical capabilities.

B) **Bond-amplified abilities** -- Individuals have magic, but bonded pairs (mates, soul-bound, sworn partners) have exponentially more. The bond acts as an amplifier. The deeper the connection, the greater the power. This creates a world where relationships are strategic resources as well as emotional ones.

C) **Court magic tied to political allegiance** -- Power flows from courts, factions, or ruling structures. Swearing allegiance grants access to magic. Changing allegiance means losing power and gaining new forms. Falling in love with someone from another court means one of them must give up everything they know.

D) **Nature or elemental magic tied to emotional state** -- Magic is drawn from the natural world, but its expression is filtered through the wielder's emotional state. A calm mage shapes water gently; a furious one creates tsunamis. A mage falling in love finds their magic becoming unpredictable, wild, and beautiful.

E) **Blood or sacrifice magic** -- Power requires giving something up. Blood, years, memories, bonds. The magical economy is one of exchange. Love becomes complicated when caring about someone means you have something precious to sacrifice -- and something precious to lose.

F) **Dream-walking or mind-linking between bonded pairs** -- Bonded individuals share a mental or dream space. They can enter each other's minds, see each other's memories, feel each other's emotions. This creates radical intimacy -- and radical vulnerability. There is no hiding in a bond like this.

G) **Other** (describe)
