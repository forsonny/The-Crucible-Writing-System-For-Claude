# Question Overrides: Dystopian/Utopian Fiction

When the Dystopian/Utopian genre is selected, the following questions receive genre-specific answer options. These replace the default fantasy-oriented options while maintaining the same question IDs, state keys, and save commands. Options marked with `[DEFAULT]` retain the original wording; all others are dystopian-specific.

Questions not listed here use their default options unchanged.

---

## Document 1: Crucible Thesis

### Q1.1 -- The Burden

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS -- Question 1 of 10**

Every Crucible story has an external burden -- something carried toward destruction or completion. In a dystopian world, what form does this burden take?

A) Forbidden knowledge that could awaken the populace -- proof that the regime's foundational narrative is a lie, suppressed history, or scientific evidence the system cannot allow to exist
B) A person the system wants eliminated -- a witness, a dissident's child, a genetic anomaly, someone whose existence disproves the regime's claims
C) Proof of the regime's crimes -- recordings, documents, testimonies that would shatter public compliance if released
D) A child who represents the old world or the new one -- someone born outside the system, raised with forbidden knowledge, or carrying genetic traits the regime fears
E) Technology that could free or further enslave -- a device, formula, or code that shifts the balance of power permanently
F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** Dystopian burdens often carry a dual nature -- they can liberate or destroy depending on who wields them. Build this ambiguity into the burden when possible. The regime wants the burden destroyed not because it is evil but because it is *destabilizing*.

### Q1.2 -- The Fire

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS -- Question 2 of 10**

The Fire is your protagonist's internal power that threatens to consume them. In a regime built on control, what burns inside them?

A) The ability to see through the regime's illusions or propaganda -- perception that cuts through manufactured reality, whether literal (seeing past holograms, resisting drugs) or cognitive (recognizing logical contradictions the system depends on)
B) A genetic trait the regime fears -- a mutation, immunity, or biological difference that makes them resistant to the system's primary control mechanism
C) Forbidden memory or knowledge of the Before Times -- they remember (or have access to memories of) the world before the regime, which gives them context no one else possesses
D) Connection to suppressed technology -- they can interface with, operate, or repair technology the regime has forbidden, giving them capabilities outside the system's model
E) Emotional capacity in a world that drugs it away -- in a society that chemically suppresses strong feeling, they feel everything, which is both a gift and an unbearable burden
F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** The Fire in dystopian fiction is often the very thing the regime was designed to suppress. This creates a powerful feedback loop: the protagonist's growth threatens the system, and the system's response forces the protagonist to grow further. The danger is not just personal corruption but *becoming visible* to the regime.

### Q1.6 -- Antagonist's Truth

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS -- Question 6 of 10**

What truth does the regime's representative see that others deny? This philosophy must be genuinely compelling -- the audience should understand why someone would believe it.

A) "Chaos is worse than control" -- the world before the regime was violent, unstable, and cruel. The system saved humanity from itself. Freedom is just a polite word for suffering.
B) "People cannot be trusted with freedom" -- given choice, humans choose destruction. They pollute, they war, they hoard. The regime is not oppression; it is parenting on a civilizational scale.
C) "Order requires the sacrifice of the few" -- yes, some suffer under the system. But billions are fed, sheltered, and safe. The math is simple. The protagonist's rebellion will kill more people than the regime ever did.
D) "The old world proved humanity needs management" -- look at what freedom produced: ecological collapse, nuclear war, pandemic, famine. The evidence is conclusive. The experiment of liberty failed.
E) "Equality requires uniformity" -- difference creates hierarchy. Hierarchy creates suffering. The only way to achieve true equality is to make everyone the same. The protagonist's uniqueness is not a gift; it is a threat to everyone else's peace.
F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** The strongest dystopian antagonists are right about the problem and wrong about the solution. Each of these truths contains a genuine insight about human nature or history. The protagonist must be able to answer the argument, not just defeat the arguer.

### Q4.4 -- Constellation Expansion

```
**CONSTELLATION -- Question 4 of 7**

What types of allies join the protagonist's network in Movement III?

A) Underground resistance cells -- organized, compartmentalized groups with their own leadership, agendas, and trust issues; they bring capability but also competing priorities
B) Defecting regime officials -- administrators, scientists, or military officers whose conscience (or self-interest) finally overcame their compliance; they bring knowledge but are never fully trusted
C) Outcasts and exiles -- people the system expelled, erased, or failed to capture; they survive outside the regime's boundaries and know the gaps in its surveillance
D) Smugglers and black market operators -- people who profit from the regime's contradictions; they are pragmatic, unreliable, and essential; their loyalty is transactional until the protagonist changes that
E) Preserved-knowledge keepers -- teachers, librarians, oral historians, religious figures, or archivists who have maintained forbidden knowledge in secret; they are fragile but carry irreplaceable cargo
F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** Constellation expansion in dystopian fiction creates trust dilemmas that do not exist in other genres. Every new ally could be an informant. Every cell could be compromised. The Crucible's Constellation strand should track not just who joins but how trust is established, tested, and sometimes betrayed.

## Document 8: Mercy Ledger

### Mercy Recipients -- Dystopian Options

For each of the four mercies, the recipient options are overridden:

```
**MERCY [X] -- Question 1 of 4**

Who receives this mercy?

A) A regime enforcer following orders -- a soldier, guard, or officer who was doing their job when captured or defeated
B) An informant whose betrayal was coerced -- someone who turned others in because the regime threatened their family
C) A complicit citizen who benefited from the system -- someone who looked away from the regime's crimes because the system worked for them
D) A regime scientist or architect -- someone who designed or maintained the mechanisms of control
E) A fellow resistance member who cracked under pressure and informed -- someone whose weakness cost lives
F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** Mercy in dystopian fiction is uniquely costly because the resistance has suffered at the hands of these people (or people like them). Allies will object strenuously. The protagonist's mercy challenges the resistance's moral framework, not just the regime's.

## Document 9: World Forge

### Q9.1 -- World's Wound

```
**WORLD FORGE -- Question 1 of 9**

What cosmic problem does the Quest address? In dystopian fiction, the World's Wound is the crisis that JUSTIFIED the regime -- the catastrophe that made people accept control.

A) Ecological collapse -- environmental destruction so severe that only centralized resource management could sustain human life; the regime controls water, food, air, or habitable zones
B) Pandemic -- a plague (biological, technological, or memetic) that enabled authoritarian takeover through emergency powers that were never relinquished
C) Class war -- economic inequality that escalated to open conflict, after which the winning faction established permanent control to prevent it from happening again
D) Technological catastrophe -- an AI disaster, nuclear event, or infrastructure collapse that led to either technophobia (Luddite regime) or tech-worship (technocratic regime)
E) Resource war -- conflict over dwindling resources (energy, water, rare earth, food) that created the current geopolitical order and its enforced hierarchy
F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** The World's Wound is the regime's *origin story* and its ongoing justification. "We had to do this because..." The wound must be real enough that the regime's response was understandable, even if its current form is monstrous. This gives the antagonist's philosophy its kernel of truth.

```
D) Connection to the natural world beyond the walls -- in a regime built on separation from nature, the protagonist can access, communicate with, or draw strength from the ecosystem the system sealed away
E) Pharmaceutical resistance or sensitivity -- the protagonist's body processes the regime's compliance drugs differently, either metabolizing them (rendering them immune) or being hypersensitive (experiencing what others cannot)
```

```
**WORLD FORGE -- Question 5 of 9**
```

## Integration Notes

When loading these overrides:
1. Replace the default options for each listed question with the dystopian versions above
2. Preserve the question numbering, state keys, and save commands from the default system
3. The `[Design note]` blocks should be displayed to the user as context, not as answer options
4. If the user selects "Other (describe)" for any overridden question, their custom answer takes precedence
