# Fantasy Genre Rules

Genre conventions, prose guidelines, and craft rules for Epic Fantasy within the Crucible Writing System. These rules apply alongside the base Crucible Structure (36 beats, 3 strands, 5 Forge Points, Mercy Engine, Dark Mirror).

---

## Genre Expectations

Fantasy readers arrive with specific expectations. Meeting them well is not formula -- it is fluency in the genre's language.

### What Readers Expect

- **Complex Magic Systems:** Power with rules, cost, and consequence. Magic that feels wondrous on first encounter and dangerous on the tenth.
- **Multi-Layered World-Building:** A world that feels lived-in, with history, cultures, economies, and ecologies that extend beyond the protagonist's immediate experience.
- **Character Growth Through Adversity:** Protagonists shaped by suffering, not just empowered by it. The Fire Strand carries enormous weight in fantasy because power transformation is often literal.
- **Themes of Good vs. Evil with Nuance:** The best fantasy resists pure black-and-white morality. The Dark Mirror's "Kernel of Truth" requirement maps directly to this -- antagonists must have comprehensible philosophy, not cartoon villainy.
- **High Stakes:** Fantasy permits and often demands world-level stakes. The Quest Strand should escalate from personal to cosmic, and readers expect the escalation to feel earned.
- **Sense of Wonder:** Even in dark fantasy, there must be moments of awe. The world should contain things that make characters (and readers) stop and marvel.

### What Readers Will Not Forgive

- Magic without cost (violates both genre convention and the Fire Strand's structural role)
- World-building that contradicts itself
- Evil for evil's sake (the Dark Mirror requirement prevents this, but be vigilant)
- Modern sensibilities in ancient settings without acknowledgment or intention
- Prophecy that removes agency (prophecy should constrain, not determine)
- Deus ex machina endings (the Mercy Engine exists specifically to prevent this -- victory flows through earlier choices)

## Prose Guidelines

### Voice and Register

Fantasy prose occupies a spectrum from the elevated (Tolkien, Le Guin) to the colloquial (Abercrombie, Hobb). Choose your register deliberately and maintain it.

**Elevated Register:**
- Longer sentences, subordinate clauses, measured rhythm
- Suitable for epic, high fantasy, mythic fantasy
- Risk: purple prose, reader fatigue
- Example authors: Tolkien, McKillip, Kay

**Middle Register:**
- Natural but not contemporary, varied sentence length
- Suitable for most fantasy subgenres
- Risk: blandness if not distinctive
- Example authors: Le Guin, Sanderson, Hobb

**Grounded Register:**
- Short, direct, modern-adjacent phrasing
- Suitable for grimdark, low fantasy, urban fantasy
- Risk: breaking immersion in secondary worlds
- Example authors: Abercrombie, Lawrence, Lynch

### Avoiding Modern Idioms in Pseudo-Medieval Settings

This is the single most common prose failure in fantasy. Modern idioms shatter immersion in secondary-world settings.

**Expressions to Avoid in Secondary-World Fantasy:**

| Modern Expression | Problem | Alternative |
|-------------------|---------|-------------|
| "Okay" | 20th-century American | "Very well," "Agreed," or simply a nod |
| "Guys" | Modern casual | "Men," "Companions," "You lot" |
| "No way" | Contemporary slang | "Impossible," "It cannot be" |
| "Got it" | Modern shorthand | "I understand," "Understood" |
| "Stressed out" | Modern psychology | "Burdened," "Worn thin" |
| "Paranoid" | Clinical terminology | "Wary," "Mistrustful," "Sees enemies in every shadow" |
| "Triggered" | Contemporary jargon | "Provoked," "Goaded," "Set alight" |
| "Literally" (as emphasis) | Modern misuse | Remove or use "truly," "in truth" |
| "I feel like..." | Modern hedging | "I believe," "It seems to me" |
| "Kind of" / "Sort of" | Modern filler | Remove or restructure |

**The Test:** If the phrase would sound natural in a modern coffee shop but jarring in a stone hall, cut it.

**Exception:** If your world has a deliberately modern or anachronistic tone (as in some low fantasy or comedic fantasy), anachronisms become a style choice rather than an error. Declare this in the style profile and apply consistently.

### Balancing Action with Introspection

Fantasy has a unique challenge: magic systems and action sequences are exciting, but the Fire Strand demands internal transformation. Pure action without reflection produces hollow spectacle; pure introspection without action produces stasis.

**The Rhythm:**
- After every major action sequence, allow at least one scene of reflection
- After every major internal realization, create external consequence within two scenes
- During action, weave in brief moments of internal state (a sentence or two, not paragraphs)
- During introspection, ground in sensory detail to prevent abstraction

**Beats That Demand Balance:**
- Forge Points require all three strands simultaneously -- action, internal crisis, and relational crisis must interweave within the same scenes
- Mercy moments are inherently internal decisions made during external pressure
- The Apex (Willed Surrender) is the ultimate fusion: an internal choice that resolves external conflict

### Description and World-Building in Prose

- **First encounter:** Describe fully. When the protagonist enters a new city, forest, or magical space for the first time, invest in sensory immersion.
- **Return visits:** Describe only what has changed. Readers remember; trust them.
- **Magic in action:** Focus on sensation and cost, not mechanics. Save mechanical explanation for quieter moments.
- **Exposition:** Never more than three paragraphs of world-building without character reaction or action. If information must be conveyed, filter it through character experience.

## Dialogue Rules

### Register by Character Type

| Character Type | Speech Pattern | Example |
|----------------|---------------|---------|
| Nobility/Royalty | Formal, measured, politically careful | "I would have you consider the implications before you commit this court to war." |
| Scholars/Mages | Precise, technical when discussing craft, distant | "The resonance frequency of a binding ward depends on the caster's intent, not their power." |
| Soldiers/Warriors | Direct, practical, dark humor | "We hold the wall or we die behind it. Either way, I need more arrows." |
| Common Folk | Colloquial within the world's idiom, practical | "Harvest won't wait for wars, lord or no lord." |
| Priests/Mystics | Allusive, metaphorical, sometimes maddeningly indirect | "The river does not choose its course. It follows the land that was shaped before it." |
| Children | Simple vocabulary, concrete thinking, occasional startling insight | "If he can do magic, why is he afraid?" |

### Anachronistic Expressions to Avoid in Dialogue

Beyond the general prose list, these are especially jarring in character speech:

- **"Shut up"** -- Use "Be silent," "Hold your tongue," "Enough" (register-dependent)
- **"What the hell"** -- Use "What in the gods' names," "By the Forge," or setting-specific oaths
- **"I don't care"** -- Use "It matters nothing to me," "Let it be," "I've no stake in it"
- **"That's crazy"** -- Use "That's madness," "You've lost your reason"
- **"Thanks"** -- Use "My thanks," "I am grateful," "You have my gratitude" (for formal); "I owe you" (for casual)
- **"Sorry"** -- Use "Forgive me," "I beg pardon," "I regret" (formal); "My fault," "I should not have" (casual)
- **"Sure"** -- Use "Of course," "Certainly," "As you say"

### Oaths and Exclamations

Create setting-specific oaths tied to your world's religion, magic system, or history. These do enormous work for immersion with minimal effort.

**Examples of World-Specific Oaths:**
- By gods/deities: "By Aethon's flame," "Gods below"
- By magic system: "Forge and fire," "By the binding"
- By cultural touchstone: "On my blood," "By the old compact"
- Mild oaths (equivalent to "damn"): reference minor spirits, weather, animals
- Strong oaths (equivalent to profanity): reference death, corruption, dark powers

### Formal vs. Informal Register in Dialogue

Characters should shift register based on context:
- **Public speech** should be more formal than private conversation
- **Stress/danger** should strip formality -- even nobles curse in battle
- **Intimacy** should lower register -- lovers and close friends speak plainly
- **Deception** often raises register -- characters being formal to hide something

## Magic System Rules

### Sanderson's Laws Applied to Crucible Fantasy

Brandon Sanderson's Laws of Magic provide the most practical framework for magic system design in fiction. Here is how they apply within the Crucible Structure.

#### First Law: Resolution and Understanding

> An author's ability to solve problems with magic in a satisfying way is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic.

**Crucible Application:**
- The Quest Strand cannot be resolved by magic the reader does not understand
- Forge Points that involve magical solutions must use established rules
- The Apex Surrender, if it involves magical power, must feel earned through previously demonstrated mechanics
- Beat 31 (Unexpected Agents) can use magic -- but only magic whose rules have been shown

**Practical Rule:** If magic solves a problem in the final third of the book, its rules must be established in the first third.

#### Second Law: Limitations and Costs

> Limitations are more interesting than powers. What a character cannot do is more important than what they can.

**Crucible Application:**
- The Fire Strand IS this law in narrative form. The protagonist's power must have cost, limitation, and danger.
- Every use of magic should register on the Mercy Engine -- does the protagonist show restraint, or does power corrupt?
- The Dark Mirror often represents what happens when limitations are rejected
- Power without limitation produces no dramatic tension and undermines every Forge Point

**The Cost Framework:**
| Cost Type | Description | Crucible Strand Affected |
|-----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Physical | Pain, exhaustion, aging, injury | Fire (body as battleground) |
| Psychological | Memory loss, emotional numbing, paranoia | Fire (identity erosion) |
| Relational | Alienation, fear from others, isolation | Constellation (bonds strained) |
| Moral | Corruption, loss of empathy, cruelty creep | Fire + Constellation |
| Material | Rare components consumed, tools destroyed | Quest (resource management) |
| Temporal | Time lost, shortened lifespan, accelerated aging | Fire + Quest |

#### Third Law: Expand Before You Add

> Before adding a new magic system element, see if you can expand what you already have.

**Crucible Application:**
- Resist introducing new magical mechanics after Movement II
- In Movements III-V, deepen existing magic rather than broadening it
- The protagonist's Fire should evolve in understanding, not accumulate new unrelated powers
- If the magic system branches, each branch should reflect a strand (Quest-functional magic, Fire-transformative magic, Constellation-relational magic)

### Hard vs. Soft Magic in the Crucible

**Hard Magic** (well-defined rules, clear costs):
- Best for: Quest resolution, Forge Point mechanics, action sequences
- Sanderson's First Law applies strongly
- Readers can anticipate and appreciate clever use
- Risk: over-explanation kills wonder

**Soft Magic** (mysterious, unexplained, numinous):
- Best for: atmosphere, the Dark Mirror's power, world-building texture, the sense of wonder
- Sanderson's First Law applies lightly -- soft magic creates problems more than it solves them
- Readers experience awe rather than understanding
- Risk: deus ex machina if used to resolve conflicts

**The Recommended Approach:** Most Crucible fantasy benefits from a hybrid. The protagonist's Fire should be relatively hard (readers understand the cost and mechanics), while the wider world contains soft magic that preserves mystery. The Dark Mirror may wield magic that operates on different, less understood principles -- making them more threatening.

### Magic and the Mercy Engine

Magic interacts with the Mercy Engine in specific ways:
- **Mercy often means NOT using power** when it would solve a problem
- The cost of magical restraint should be tangible -- enemies escape, battles are lost, suffering continues
- Recipients of mercy may later provide non-magical solutions, reinforcing the theme that power alone is insufficient
- The Fourth Mercy (The Impossible Gift) gains extraordinary weight when the protagonist could use overwhelming magical force but chooses grace instead

## Fantasy Tropes: Use Well vs. Avoid

### Tropes to Use Well

These are genre staples that work when executed with craft:

| Trope | How to Use Well | Crucible Connection |
|-------|-----------------|---------------------|
| The Chosen One | Give the "chosen" status a cost, not just a destiny. Make it a burden, not a gift. | Quest Strand: the burden IS the chosen status |
| The Fellowship | Make each member essential AND flawed. Bonds should fracture and reform. | Constellation Strand: the fellowship IS the constellation |
| The Mentor's Death | Earn it. The mentor must be fully realized, not just a plot device to die. | FP0 or FP3: mentor death can ignite or enable the finale |
| Ancient Prophecy | Prophecy should constrain, not determine. Leave room for interpretation and agency. | Quest Strand: prophecy as burden, not roadmap |
| The Dark Lord | Give them comprehensible motivation. The Dark Mirror requirement does this automatically. | Dark Mirror: the antagonist IS the shadow self |
| The Magic Sword | Make the weapon a symbol, not a solution. Its power should come with cost. | Fire Strand: the weapon as externalized Fire |
| The Hidden Heritage | Revelation should complicate, not simplify. Learning you are royalty does not solve problems; it creates them. | Fire Strand: heritage as the source of dangerous power |
| Training Montage | Show what is learned AND what is sacrificed during training. Cost, not just skill growth. | Fire Strand + Constellation: training costs time, relationships, innocence |

### Tropes to Handle with Extreme Care

| Trope | The Problem | If You Must Use It |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| Prophecy That Removes Choice | Undermines character agency -- the Apex requires WILLED surrender | Make prophecy ambiguous, subject to interpretation, or only partially accurate |
| Resurrection/Return from Death | Cheapens FP3's death requirement and the Mercy Engine | If used, the returned character must be fundamentally changed -- death should leave marks |
| Power Levels as Video Game Stats | Reduces Fire Strand to accumulation rather than transformation | Focus on what power costs and how it changes the wielder, not numerical strength |
| Info-Dump World-Building | Breaks narrative momentum in every strand | Filter through character experience; reveal through action, not lecture |
| Love Triangle as Primary Conflict | Reduces Constellation to romantic drama | Love triangles can exist but should not be the central relational tension |
| Evil Races/Species | Reduces moral complexity, undermines Dark Mirror's comprehensible philosophy | Individual choices, not biology, should determine alignment |
| Instant Mastery | Undercuts the Fire Strand's arc of growth through suffering | Mastery should take the entire story and arrive at the Apex, not before |
| Magical MacGuffin That Solves Everything | Violates the Mercy Engine (victory through power alone) | Objects of power should create problems as often as they solve them |

### Tropes to Actively Avoid

| Trope | Why It Fails in the Crucible |
|-------|------------------------------|
| Damsel in Distress (as sole purpose for female character) | Constellation members must have their own agency and arcs |
| Might Makes Right (unexamined) | The Mercy Engine exists specifically to subvert this |
| Convenient Amnesia | Memory loss as a cheap mystery device; if used, it must carry real weight |
| "The Real Treasure Was the Friends We Made Along the Way" (unearned) | The Constellation must be tested, fractured, and rebuilt -- not simply invoked |
| Villain Monologue Explaining Plan | The Dark Mirror's offer (Beat 26) should be a genuine temptation, not an explanation |

## Subgenre Notes

The Crucible Structure adapts to all fantasy subgenres, but each has distinct conventions that affect strand emphasis and tone.

### Epic Fantasy

**Characteristics:** Large scope, multiple POVs possible, world-changing stakes, extensive world-building, series-length narratives.

**Strand Emphasis:** All three strands carry equal weight. The Quest Strand often has multiple layers (personal quest nested within world-saving quest). The Constellation tends to be large.

**Crucible Notes:**
- Forge Points may span multiple chapters
- The Mercy Engine has room for more than four mercies (but four remain required)
- World-building can be deeper -- readers expect investment
- Series planning: each book can have its own Forge Points while the series has meta-Forge Points

**Tone:** Ranges from hopeful (Tolkien, Sanderson) to grim (Martin, Cook). Choose and commit.

### High Fantasy

**Characteristics:** Secondary world entirely, no connection to our reality. Emphasis on wonder and the numinous. Good and evil are often metaphysical forces.

**Strand Emphasis:** Fire Strand is paramount -- magic and transformation are literal, not metaphorical. The Dark Mirror often represents cosmic evil given personal form.

**Crucible Notes:**
- The sense of wonder requirement is highest here
- Magic systems can be softer without losing reader trust
- The Apex often has genuine metaphysical weight
- Theme statements tend toward universal truths

### Low Fantasy

**Characteristics:** Our world (or a world very like it) with subtle magic. Emphasis on politics, economics, and human conflict. Magic is rare, feared, or hidden.

**Strand Emphasis:** Quest and Constellation dominate. Fire Strand may be more metaphorical -- the "power" that corrupts might be political rather than magical.

**Crucible Notes:**
- Magic scarcity increases its dramatic weight when it does appear
- Forge Points are often political or military rather than magical
- The Dark Mirror is more human, less archetypal
- Mercy moments hit harder in worlds where mercy is rare

### Dark Fantasy

**Characteristics:** Horror-adjacent, morally ambiguous, high cost for everything. The world is hostile and indifferent. Victory is costly and incomplete.

**Strand Emphasis:** Fire Strand dominates. Corruption is the central threat, and the line between protagonist and Dark Mirror is thin.

**Crucible Notes:**
- The Mercy Engine becomes more difficult and more important -- mercy in a cruel world is radical
- FP3's death may be especially brutal
- The Apex may involve a pyrrhic victory -- the protagonist wins but at devastating cost
- Avoid nihilism: even in dark fantasy, the Mercy Engine insists that compassion matters

### Grimdark

**Characteristics:** The darkest end of the spectrum. Moral ambiguity is the norm. "Heroes" do terrible things. Victory and defeat are relative terms.

**Strand Emphasis:** All strands are under maximum stress at all times. The Constellation may be unstable throughout.

**Crucible Notes:**
- The Crucible Structure is especially valuable here because it prevents grimdark from collapsing into nihilism
- The Mercy Engine is the structural mechanism that maintains hope in a hopeless world
- The Dark Mirror may be uncomfortably close to the protagonist
- The Apex Surrender may be the protagonist's only genuinely heroic act
- Careful: grimdark readers still need someone to root for. The Lie and Wound from the Constellation Bible create that sympathy

### Heroic Fantasy / Sword and Sorcery

**Characteristics:** Focus on individual heroics, adventure-driven, often episodic. Lighter tone, emphasis on action and charisma.

**Strand Emphasis:** Quest Strand leads. The protagonist's competence is a feature, not a flaw.

**Crucible Notes:**
- Fire Strand may be less about corruption and more about the cost of the heroic life
- Constellation often features a small, tight-knit band rather than a sprawling network
- Forge Points can be more action-driven, but emotional weight must still be present
- The Mercy Engine prevents "hero solves everything through strength" narratives
- Tone can be lighter -- humor is permitted and even expected

## Fantasy-Specific Checklist

Before beginning the planning questionnaire, verify:

- [ ] Magic system has defined costs and limitations
- [ ] World-building extends beyond what the story requires (for depth), but only what the story requires appears on page
- [ ] Dialogue register matches character types and shifts with context
- [ ] Modern idioms have been identified and eliminated (or deliberately retained with justification)
- [ ] The Dark Mirror's philosophy reflects a genuine fantasy tradition (power, immortality, restoration of lost glory, etc.)
- [ ] The Mercy Engine's recipients are meaningful within the fantasy context (not just generic "enemy soldiers")
- [ ] Forge Points engage the magic system, not just the plot
- [ ] The Apex Surrender involves the protagonist's relationship to power, not just external conflict
- [ ] Prophecy (if present) constrains but does not determine
- [ ] Subgenre conventions are identified and respected
