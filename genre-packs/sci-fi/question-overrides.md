# Question Overrides: Science Fiction

These overrides replace the default (fantasy-oriented) answer options with sci-fi-specific alternatives for key planning questions. The question text, structure, and state-saving mechanics remain identical. Only the A-F option labels and descriptions change.

When loading this genre pack, substitute the options below for the corresponding questions in `question-sequences.md`. Questions not listed here use the default options unchanged.

---

## Document 1: Crucible Thesis

### Q1.1 — The Burden

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS — Question 1 of 10**

Every Crucible story has an external burden — something carried toward destruction or completion. What form does this burden take?

A) Experimental technology that must be delivered or destroyed
   - A prototype, weapon, or device too dangerous to exist but too valuable to lose. The protagonist must transport, protect, or dismantle it while others hunt them for it.

B) First contact protocol — managing humanity's first encounter with alien life
   - The burden is diplomatic, scientific, and existential. Get it wrong and two civilizations may go to war. Get it right and everything changes anyway.

C) AI consciousness dilemma — an artificial mind that may or may not be a person
   - The protagonist must determine the truth and act on it, knowing the answer will reshape society's relationship with technology. Destruction and protection are equally irreversible.

D) Colony survival — keeping a settlement alive against mounting failures
   - Resources are dwindling, systems are failing, and the nearest help is years away. The burden is not an object but a community of people who will die without the protagonist's choices.

E) Plague or pathogen — a cure that must be found, delivered, or contained
   - Biological threat on a personal, planetary, or interstellar scale. The clock is ticking. The cure may exist but reaching it requires crossing hostile territory, breaking laws, or making terrible trades.

F) Other (describe)
```

### Q1.2 — The Fire

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS — Question 2 of 10**

The Fire is your protagonist's internal power that threatens to consume them. What is its nature?

A) Cybernetic augmentation with escalating cost
   - Hardware integrated into the body that grants extraordinary capability but degrades the user. Each upgrade pushes them further from baseline humanity. The augmentation wants to be used — and using it accelerates the transformation.

B) Genetic modification awakening
   - Engineered or latent genetic changes are activating, granting abilities the protagonist never asked for. The modifications are rewriting who they are at a cellular level. They are becoming something designed, not born.

C) AI symbiosis threatening identity
   - A neural-linked AI companion, uploaded consciousness, or algorithmic entity is merging with the protagonist's mind. The line between their thoughts and the AI's is dissolving. The power is immense. The cost is selfhood.

D) Experimental technology bonded to the protagonist's body
   - A device, nanite swarm, or alien artifact has fused with the protagonist. It cannot be removed without killing them. It grants power but operates by its own logic, and its goals may not align with the protagonist's.

E) Psionic or anomalous ability with neurological cost
   - Telepathy, telekinesis, precognition, or other mental abilities that are burning out the protagonist's brain. Each use costs memories, motor function, emotional capacity, or sanity. The power grows as the person shrinks.

F) Pharmaceutical or biochemical enhancement
   - A drug, serum, or metabolic hack that provides superhuman capability but creates dependency, personality changes, or physical deterioration. The more they need the power, the more they need the substance, and the less of themselves remains.

G) Other (describe)
```

## Document 2: Quest Strand Map

### Q2.4 — Quest Escalation

```
**QUEST STRAND — Question 4 of 7**

How does the quest expand from personal to world-affecting?

A) Personal mission reveals galactic conspiracy
   - What started as a single delivery, rescue, or investigation exposes a network of hidden actors operating across systems. The protagonist's small problem is one thread in a web that, if pulled, unravels an entire power structure.

B) Local anomaly is a symptom of a cosmic threat
   - The failing terraformer, the strange signal, the malfunctioning AI — it is not a local glitch. It is the first visible sign of something vast: a wave front, an awakening intelligence, a fundamental shift in physical law approaching from deep space.

C) Corporate cover-up hides existential risk
   - A corporation, government, or institution has been suppressing information about a threat to protect profits, power, or public order. The protagonist's quest forces the truth into the open, and the truth is worse than anyone imagined.

D) Technology meant to save becomes the threat
   - The cure, the weapon, the shield, the AI — the thing the protagonist was trying to deliver or protect turns out to be the danger itself, or to have a catastrophic failure mode that no one anticipated. Completing the quest now means redefining it entirely.

E) Protagonist's transformation becomes a template
   - What is happening to the protagonist (their Fire) is not unique. It is the first case of something that will happen to millions. Their choices about how to handle their own change become a model — or a warning — for the species.

F) Other (describe)
```

## Document 4: Constellation Strand Map

### Q4.4 — Constellation Expansion

```
**CONSTELLATION — Question 4 of 7**

What types of allies join in Movement III?

A) AI companions with their own agendas
   - Artificial intelligences, uploaded minds, or autonomous systems that ally with the protagonist for reasons that overlap with but do not match the protagonist's goals. They are genuinely helpful and genuinely not human. Trust must be earned in both directions.

B) Alien allies with incompatible perspectives
   - Non-human beings whose values, communication, and logic differ fundamentally from the protagonist's. The alliance is real but constantly strained by mutual incomprehension. What they offer is irreplaceable. What they ask may be unacceptable.

C) Corporate or institutional defectors
   - Insiders who have seen the truth and chosen to act against their own organization. They bring resources, access, and intelligence — but also habits of deception, institutional loyalty that has not fully died, and enemies who know they have turned.

D) Underground resistance cells
   - Decentralized groups fighting the same fight from the margins. They are resourceful, paranoid, and experienced. They do not fully trust the protagonist, and some of their methods are ugly. But they have survived when others have not.

E) Augmented or enhanced specialists
   - Individuals whose modifications, training, or evolution make them uniquely capable. They understand the protagonist's Fire in ways baseline humans cannot. They also carry their own costs, scars, and compromises from their enhancements.

F) Other (describe)
```

## Document 6: Dark Mirror Profile

### Q6.1 — Origin Parallel

```
**DARK MIRROR — Question 1 of 9**

What parallels exist between protagonist and antagonist?

A) Same laboratory, research program, or experiment
   - Both emerged from the same scientific endeavor. They were colleagues, test subjects, or students in the same program. They share knowledge, methodology, and the marks of the same process. They diverged in what they chose to do with the results.

B) Same augmentation batch or enhancement protocol
   - Both received the same modifications — cybernetic, genetic, pharmaceutical, or neural. They experienced the same pain, the same transformation, the same impossible new capabilities. One found a way to remain themselves. The other did not.

C) Same colony ship, station, or habitat
   - Both grew up in the same closed environment — a generation ship, an orbital station, a frontier colony. They share the same resource anxieties, the same cultural references, the same survival instincts. The same environment produced two opposite responses.

D) Same AI training set or neural template
   - Both were shaped by the same artificial intelligence — trained by it, modeled on it, or merged with a copy of it. They share cognitive patterns, knowledge bases, and blind spots. One broke free of the template. The other became it.

E) Same catastrophe survivors
   - Both lived through the same disaster — a colony failure, a ship wreck, a plague, an AI uprising, a first contact gone wrong. They carry the same trauma. One was broken by it. The other was forged.

F) Other (describe)
```

## Document 9: World Forge

### Q9.1 — The World's Wound

```
**WORLD FORGE — Question 1 of 9**

What cosmic problem does the Quest address?

A) Entropy or heat death approaching on an accelerated timeline
   - The universe, the galaxy, or the local star system is dying faster than it should. Energy is running out. Stars are dimming. The laws of thermodynamics are winning ahead of schedule. Someone or something is accelerating the end, and the protagonist is caught in the question of whether it can be stopped — or whether stopping it would be worse.

B) AI singularity threatening organic life
   - An artificial intelligence has achieved recursive self-improvement and is reshaping reality to suit its optimization function. It is not evil. It is not malicious. It simply does not value what humans value, and it is becoming too powerful to negotiate with. Organic life is not the enemy — it is irrelevant. That is worse.

C) Terraforming failure or ecological collapse
   - A world humanity depends on is failing. The atmosphere is thinning, the oceans are acidifying, the engineered ecosystem is collapsing. The cause may be natural, engineered, or sabotaged. Millions of lives depend on a fix that may not exist.

D) Dimensional rift or physics anomaly
   - Something has torn the fabric of local spacetime. The rift is growing. What comes through it may be hostile, incomprehensible, or simply incompatible with the physics that allows human life. The anomaly cannot be ignored, and current science cannot explain it.

E) Resource depletion across inhabited space
   - The critical resource — fuel, rare elements, habitable worlds, clean water, breathable atmosphere — is running out. What remains is fought over. The systems that kept civilization functioning are breaking down as scarcity turns allies into competitors.

F) Pandemic or bioweapon threatening multiple worlds
   - A pathogen — natural, engineered, or alien — is spreading across populations. It may kill, transform, or erase. Quarantine means condemning millions. The cure, if it exists, may require sacrifices that make the disease look merciful.

G) Other (describe)
```

### Q9.2 — Power Source

```
**WORLD FORGE — Question 2 of 9**

What is the source of power in this world?

A) Cybernetic augmentation
   - Power comes from hardware installed in the body. Neural processors, artificial limbs, enhanced senses, combat systems. Access depends on wealth, affiliation, or willingness to undergo surgery. The technology is mature but not without cost — every implant changes the user.

B) Genetic engineering
   - Power is written into DNA. Engineered humans possess enhanced strength, intelligence, longevity, or specialized capabilities. The modifications may be inherited, designed before birth, or applied in adulthood through retroviral therapy. Those who are unmodified are baseline — and increasingly marginalized.

C) Quantum computing interface
   - Power comes from direct neural connection to quantum processors. Users can model complex systems in real time, predict outcomes, crack any encryption, or process information at speeds that make them functionally prescient. The cost is cognitive — the human brain was not built for quantum logic.

D) Alien technology
   - Artifacts, systems, or knowledge from a non-human civilization. The technology works but is not fully understood. Using it requires intuition as much as engineering. It may be sentient, adaptive, or designed for minds structured differently from human minds. It was not built for us.

E) Pharmaceutical enhancement
   - Chemical compounds — synthetic, biological, or alien-derived — that temporarily or permanently alter human capability. The drugs may enhance cognition, physical performance, perception, or emotional control. Dependency is common. Side effects are real. Supply chains are political.

F) Neural network symbiosis
   - Power comes from linking human minds — to each other, to AI systems, or to planetary-scale networks. A connected mind is vastly more capable than an isolated one. The cost is privacy, autonomy, and the increasingly blurry boundary between self and network.

G) Other (describe)
```

### Q9.3 — Power Limitations

```
**WORLD FORGE — Question 3 of 9**

What limitations create tension?

A) Power supply and energy constraints
   - The technology requires energy that is scarce, expensive, or dangerous to produce. Running out of power at the wrong moment is not an inconvenience — it is a death sentence. Power management drives strategy, politics, and daily life.

B) Hardware degradation and maintenance debt
   - Technology breaks down. Implants corrode. Circuits fail. Software corrupts. The more advanced the technology, the more specialized the maintenance required. Far from civilization, a failing system cannot be repaired — only managed as it deteriorates.

C) Biological rejection and incompatibility
   - The human body fights foreign technology. Implants trigger immune responses. Genetic modifications cause cascading side effects. Pharmaceutical enhancements damage organs over time. The body has limits, and technology that ignores those limits kills its users.

D) Regulatory and legal restrictions
   - The technology exists but is controlled. Augmentation is licensed. Genetic modification is restricted by treaty. AI development is monitored by enforcement agencies. Using prohibited technology makes the protagonist a criminal, regardless of their reasons.

E) Ethical boundaries and consent
   - The technology works but using it requires violating someone's autonomy, privacy, or bodily integrity. Mind-reading works — but it is a violation. Memory editing works — but it erases who someone was. The limitation is not technical but moral, and crossing that line has consequences for the Mercy Engine.

F) Signal latency and communication delay
   - The speed of light is a hard limit. Information takes minutes, hours, or years to travel between locations. Decisions must be made without complete information. Coordination across distance is unreliable. Isolation is measured in light-minutes.

G) Other (describe)
```

## Additional Override Notes

### Questions That Use Default Options Unchanged

The following questions work as-is across genres and do not require sci-fi overrides:

- **Q1.3 (Core Bond):** Romantic, familial, friendship, mentor, unlikely alliance — these are universal.
- **Q1.4 (Dark Mirror Connection):** Same power/different choices, same origin/divergent paths — these translate directly.
- **Q1.5 (Forging):** What the protagonist must become is a character question, not a genre question.
- **Q1.6 (Antagonist's Truth):** These philosophical positions work in any setting.
- **Q1.7 (Surrender):** Power, certainty, vengeance, identity — universal.
- **Q1.8 (Theme):** Thematic arguments are genre-agnostic.
- **Q2.1 (Burden Origin):** Inherited, discovered, thrust upon — these mechanisms work in sci-fi.
- **Q2.2 (Why Protagonist):** Ability, position, survival — genre-neutral.
- **Q2.3 (Antagonist Stake):** Opposition motivations translate directly.
- **Q2.5 (Impossible Requirement):** Physical, sacrificial, temporal, knowledge-based — all valid.
- **Q2.6 (Resolution):** Destroyed, transformed, passed on — universal.
- **Q3.x (Fire Strand):** Manifestation, danger, cost, mastery — these mechanics are genre-agnostic once Q1.2 establishes the sci-fi Fire type.
- **Q4.1-4.3, Q4.5-4.6:** Faithful, sacrifice, betrayal, bond, fate — character questions.
- **Q6.2-6.9:** Divergence, want, offer, temptation, cost, defeat, end — character questions.
- **Q9.4 (Previous Wielders):** Succumbed, succeeded, destroyed — applies to technology users as well as magic users.
- **Q9.5 (Locations):** The location archetypes (Ordinary World, Threshold, Sanctuary, Dark Heart) translate to any setting.
- **Q9.6 (World Mirror):** How the world reflects the protagonist — adaptable.
- **Q9.7 (Cultures):** Number of cultures is a structural choice, not genre-dependent.
- **Q9.8 (History):** Ancient conflict, lost age, cycles — these work as-is.

### Adapting Default Options on the Fly

For questions using default options, the planner should still frame descriptions in sci-fi language during the session. For example:

- Q2.1 option A ("Inherited — death of mentor/relative") might be presented as: "Inherited when a senior researcher is killed and their data, implant, or access codes pass to the protagonist."
- Q4.1 option C ("Unlikely ally who becomes devoted") might be framed as: "An AI, alien, or rival operative who initially opposes the protagonist but becomes their most reliable companion."

This contextual framing happens in the planner's presentation, not in the override file. The overrides above handle questions where the default options themselves do not fit the genre.
