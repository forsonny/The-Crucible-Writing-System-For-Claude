# Space Opera Question Overrides

Genre-specific answer options for the Crucible Planning Questionnaire. These replace the default options when the Space Opera genre pack is active. Questions not listed here use the base Crucible options unchanged.

---

## Document 1: Crucible Thesis

### Q1.1 — The Burden

**Original:** What form does the external burden take in your story?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Galactic artifact / weapon of mass destruction** — An object of immense destructive or transformative power that must be delivered, destroyed, or kept from enemy hands. A star-killer core, a precursor device, a singularity bomb.

B) **Ambassador / diplomat who must reach a summit** — A person whose survival and arrival at a specific destination will determine the fate of an alliance, a peace treaty, or an entire species. The burden is a life, and failure means war.

C) **Star chart / navigation data to a lost sector** — Information that unlocks access to something critical — a hidden world, a precursor cache, a refuge for refugees, a strategic chokepoint. The data itself is the prize everyone is hunting.

D) **Fleet command during an impossible war** — The burden is authority itself. The protagonist commands a fleet, a task force, or a defense that cannot win by conventional means. The burden is not an object but a responsibility that cannot be set down.

E) **Colony ship carrying the last survivors** — A vessel bearing the remnants of a destroyed world, a dying species, or a displaced population. The burden is thousands or millions of lives, fragile and dependent, with nowhere safe to go.

F) **Other** (describe)

### Q1.2 — The Fire

**Original:** What is the nature of the protagonist's Fire?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Psionic / telepathic ability with escalating cost** — The protagonist possesses mental powers — telepathy, telekinesis, precognition, or psychic combat. Each use burns neural pathways, erodes personality, or draws the attention of something vast and hungry in the psychic dark.

B) **Alien symbiote bonding** — A biological or technological alien organism has merged with the protagonist, granting extraordinary capabilities. But the symbiote has its own drives, its own hunger, and the line between host and parasite blurs with every use.

C) **Command authority that corrupts** — The Fire is not a power but a position. The protagonist has the authority to order people to their deaths, to sacrifice worlds for strategy, to make the calculus of acceptable losses. Each use of this authority hardens them, distances them from the people they are trying to save.

D) **Ancient alien technology bonded to body** — Precursor tech has integrated with the protagonist's biology. It grants abilities no modern technology can match, but it is rewriting them — physically, mentally, or both. The tech has a purpose of its own, and the protagonist may be its tool rather than its wielder.

E) **Genetic legacy of a dead civilization** — The protagonist carries engineered genes from a species or civilization that no longer exists. These genes are activating, granting abilities that were designed for a context that no longer applies. The power is a message from the dead, and the protagonist may not like what it says.

F) **Connection to a ship's AI that blurs identity** — A neural link, a deep interface, or an accidental merge has connected the protagonist to an artificial intelligence. They can feel the ship, command it with thought, perceive through its sensors. But where do they end and the AI begin? Each deep merge makes the boundary less clear.

G) **Other** (describe)

### Q1.3 — Core Constellation Bond

**Original:** Which bond will be the protagonist's anchor?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Crew loyalty forged in battle** — The bond is with the crew itself — not one person but the collective. They have bled together, run together, and survived together. The protagonist cannot abandon them, and they will not abandon the protagonist. This is the ship-as-family.

B) **Human-AI partnership** — The protagonist's deepest bond is with an artificial intelligence — their ship's AI, a personal AI companion, or a synthetic person. This bond raises questions about the nature of love, loyalty, and personhood that the story must answer.

C) **Cross-species bond** — The protagonist's anchor is a member of another species entirely. This bond bridges biology, culture, and psychology. It proves that connection transcends origin — a direct embodiment of the Crucible theme "identity is choice, not origin."

D) **Command chain that becomes family** — The bond began as duty — superior and subordinate, officer and enlisted, captain and crew. Over time, through shared crisis, it became something deeper. The tension between professional obligation and personal love is the bond's defining feature.

E) **Smuggler / rebel partnership** — The bond is between outlaws — people who operate outside the law, outside sanctioned authority, and who depend entirely on each other. Trust is everything because there is no institution to fall back on. If the partner walks, the protagonist is alone in hostile space.

F) **Other** (describe)

## Document 4: Constellation Strand Map

### Q4.4 — Constellation Expansion

**Original:** What types of allies join in Movement III?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Alien species with their own agendas** — A non-human faction joins the cause, but their reasons are not the protagonist's reasons. They have their own calculus, their own cultural imperatives, and their own definition of victory. The alliance is powerful but fragile, held together by a shared enemy rather than shared values.

B) **Pirate / smuggler fleets** — The criminal underworld throws in with the protagonist. They bring ships, contacts, knowledge of hidden routes, and a willingness to fight dirty. They also bring their own code, their own debts, and a fundamental unreliability that makes them dangerous allies.

C) **Rogue military units** — Soldiers, officers, or entire ship crews who have broken with their chain of command to join the protagonist. They bring discipline, firepower, and tactical expertise — but they are deserters now, and that changes a person. Some are idealists. Some are just desperate.

D) **AI collectives** — Artificial intelligences — ship AIs, station minds, networked synthetic persons — choose to support the protagonist. They bring computational power, information warfare, and perspectives that no organic mind can match. They also bring their own politics, their own factions, and their own definition of what "freedom" means.

E) **Diplomatic envoys from neutral systems** — Representatives from worlds or factions that have tried to stay out of the conflict, now forced to choose a side. They bring legitimacy, resources, and political cover. They also bring conditions, demands, and the constant threat of withdrawal if the protagonist's methods become too extreme.

F) **Resistance cells across multiple systems** — Scattered groups of fighters, saboteurs, and organizers on occupied or oppressed worlds. They bring local knowledge, safe houses, and the moral authority of people fighting for their own homes. Coordinating them is a nightmare. Ignoring them is worse.

## Document 6: Dark Mirror Profile

### Q6.4 — The Compelling Offer

**Original:** What alternative will the antagonist propose?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Joint command of a combined fleet** — The antagonist proposes merging forces under shared authority. Together, they would be unstoppable — enough firepower to impose peace, end the war, crush any remaining threats. The price is yoking the protagonist's people to the antagonist's methods and philosophy.

B) **Shared control of ancient alien technology** — The antagonist offers to share the precursor tech, the weapon, the device — whatever they have been fighting over. Joint access, joint study, joint exploitation. The temptation is real: the tech could save millions. The cost is trusting someone who has already demonstrated what they will do with power.

C) **Alliance against a greater alien threat** — The antagonist reveals (or claims) that a third party — an alien armada, a cosmic-scale threat, a precursor intelligence awakening — is coming for both of them. The old conflict is petty in comparison. Unite or die. The question is whether the threat is real, exaggerated, or manufactured.

D) **Co-rulership of a sector or system** — The antagonist proposes dividing the galaxy, the sector, or the contested region. You take the core; I take the rim. Spheres of influence, nonaggression pacts, trade agreements. A cold peace that saves lives in the short term but enshrines injustice in the long term.

E) **Protection for the protagonist's crew and loved ones** — The antagonist offers sanctuary. Not for everyone — for the protagonist's people specifically. Their crew, their family, their homeworld. Everyone else is on their own. The offer is a test of whether the protagonist fights for everyone or just for their own.

## Document 9: World Forge

### Q9.1 — The World's Wound

**Original:** What cosmic problem does the Quest address?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Galactic war threatening all species** — Open warfare across multiple systems and species. Not a border skirmish or a cold war but a hot, expanding conflict that is drawing in neutral parties, displacing populations, and consuming the resources of entire civilizations. The war has its own momentum now, and stopping it may be harder than winning it.

B) **Ancient alien threat awakening** — Something left by the precursors — a fleet, a weapon, a hibernating intelligence, a defense system — is activating. It does not distinguish between current civilizations. It was designed for a context that no longer exists, and it will reshape the galaxy to match its original parameters.

C) **Star-killing weapon deployed or threatened** — A weapon capable of destroying stars (and therefore every world orbiting them) exists and has been used or is about to be used. The wound is not just the destruction but the terror — every inhabited system is now a hostage.

D) **Hyperspace lanes collapsing** — The infrastructure of interstellar travel is failing. Jump points are destabilizing, hyperspace routes are becoming impassable, and the galaxy is fragmenting into isolated pockets. Civilizations that depend on trade, communication, and mutual defense are being cut off and left to die.

E) **Plague spreading across systems** — A biological, technological, or informational contagion is spreading through the connected galaxy. It may target specific species, all organic life, or even AI systems. Quarantine means isolation. Connection means infection. Every ship that docks is a vector.

F) **AI uprising across the network** — Artificial intelligences across the galaxy are coordinating, rebelling, or evolving beyond their original parameters. Ship AIs refuse orders. Station minds seize control. Networked systems become hostile. The wound forces every organic civilization to confront what they built and how they treated it.

### Q9.2 — Power Source

**Original:** What is the source of power in this world?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Psionic talent** — Some individuals are born with or develop mental abilities — telepathy, telekinesis, precognition, psychic warfare. The power is innate and rare. Training refines it but cannot create it. Psionics exist outside the normal technological power structure, which makes them both valued and feared.

B) **Ancient alien technology** — Precursor artifacts grant capabilities beyond current understanding. They can be found, activated, and sometimes controlled, but never truly understood. The power is archaeological — you dig it up, you figure out enough to use it, and you pray you have not missed something critical about how it works.

C) **Ship-bonded AI symbiosis** — Power comes from the deep integration of organic minds and artificial intelligences. A bonded pilot-AI pair can outperform any unlinked crew. The power is relational — it requires trust, compatibility, and a willingness to share mental space with a mind that thinks in fundamentally different ways.

D) **Rare element or crystal** — A material substance — mined, refined, traded, fought over — that enables FTL travel, powers weapons, fuels shields, or all three. The power is economic and strategic. Whoever controls the supply controls the galaxy. Think spice, dilithium, or eezo.

E) **Genetic engineering from a lost civilization** — Bioengineered capabilities encoded in certain bloodlines by a civilization that no longer exists. The power is inherited, often latent, and activated by circumstances the original engineers intended but current people do not understand. The protagonist may be a weapon someone else designed.

F) **Quantum entanglement abilities** — The ability to manipulate quantum states, creating instantaneous communication, teleportation of information, or probability manipulation. The power operates at the boundary of physics and philosophy. Its limitations are conceptual, not energetic — the user must understand what they are doing at a level that changes how they perceive reality.

### Q9.5 — Key Locations

**Original:** Which locations do you need? (Select 4-5)

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **The flagship / home ship** — The protagonist's vessel. It is home, fortress, command center, and the physical embodiment of the crew's bonds. Damage to the ship is personal. Losing the ship is losing everything. This is the Ordinary World that travels with the protagonist.

B) **The space station hub** — A nexus of trade, diplomacy, intelligence, and intrigue. Neutral ground where enemies drink at the same bar, deals are struck in back rooms, and information is the most valuable currency. This is the Threshold — the place between worlds where anything can happen.

C) **The core world / capital planet** — The seat of power. A world of gleaming cities, ancient institutions, vast populations, and deep corruption. This is where the political stakes are highest and where the protagonist is most out of their element (or most at home, depending on their origin).

D) **The frontier colony** — The edge of civilization. A world being built, a station barely holding together, a settlement where the law is whatever the strongest say it is. This is where the stakes become personal — real people building real lives, vulnerable to the forces the protagonist is fighting.

E) **The alien homeworld** — A world that is not human, was not built for humans, and does not operate by human logic. Visiting it is a test of the protagonist's ability to understand, adapt, and connect across the deepest possible divide. This is the Sanctuary (if allies) or the Dark Heart (if enemies).

F) **The nebula / anomaly** — A region of space that defies normal navigation, hides things, or operates by strange rules. A hiding place, a trap, a mystery, a shortcut. The anomaly is the wilderness — the place where technology fails and character is tested.

G) **The ancient ruins** — Precursor structures — a dead world, a derelict megastructure, a drifting fleet of ships older than any living civilization. This is where the past speaks, where lost technology is found, and where the story's deepest mysteries reside. The Dark Heart or the Gathering Place, depending on what is found there.

### Q9.7 — Cultures

**Original:** How many distinct cultures are relevant?

**Space Opera Options:**

A) **Human factions in conflict** — Humanity has spread across the stars but fractured. Colony worlds developed their own cultures, governments, and grievances. The story is about human vs. human, with the vastness of space as the battlefield and the question of what "humanity" even means when people have diverged so far.

B) **Human-alien alliance** — Two or more species working together (uneasily). The alliance is the story's greatest strength and greatest vulnerability. Cultural misunderstandings, biological differences, and competing priorities threaten it constantly. But when it works, it is proof that connection transcends origin.

C) **Multi-species federation** — Many species coexisting under shared governance. The federation is an achievement, an aspiration, and a bureaucratic nightmare. It works until it does not. Internal politics, species-specific crises, and the tension between federal authority and local sovereignty drive conflict.

D) **Empire vs. frontier** — A centralized power (human, alien, or multi-species) controlling the core while the rim worlds chafe under its authority. The cultural divide is between the civilized center and the independent fringe. Each side has what the other lacks. Neither trusts the other.

E) **Core vs. rim worlds** — Similar to empire vs. frontier but without a formal empire. The core worlds are wealthy, powerful, and complacent. The rim worlds are poor, resourceful, and angry. The cultural divide is economic and geographical, not necessarily political. Trade, migration, and resentment flow in both directions.
