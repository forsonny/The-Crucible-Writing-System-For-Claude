# Question Overrides: Alternate History

When the Alternate History genre is selected, the following questions receive genre-specific answer options. These replace the default fantasy-oriented options while maintaining the same question IDs, state keys, and save commands. Questions not listed here use their default options unchanged.

---

## Document 1: Crucible Thesis

### Q1.1 -- The Burden

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS -- Question 1 of 10**

Every Crucible story has an external burden -- something carried toward destruction or completion. In an alternate history, what form does this burden take?

A) Knowledge of what history "should" have been
   - The protagonist possesses or discovers proof that the world diverged from a "true" timeline -- documents, memories, artifacts, or scientific evidence that the present was engineered or deflected. This knowledge is dangerous because the powers that benefit from the current timeline will kill to suppress it.

B) An artifact from the divergence point
   - A physical object that survived from the moment of divergence: the unsigned treaty, the bullet that missed, the suppressed invention, the letter that was never sent. It is proof that things were meant to go differently, and possessing it makes the protagonist a target.

C) A person who embodies the old/true timeline
   - Someone who should not exist in this timeline -- a descendant of a figure who died before the divergence in our history, a person with memories of the "other" world, or a child whose very genetics prove the official history is a lie. The protagonist must protect this person while understanding what they represent.

D) A mission to correct or preserve the altered timeline
   - The protagonist has been tasked (or has tasked themselves) with either restoring history to its "correct" course or preventing others from tampering with it further. The burden is the mission itself -- its moral weight, its practical impossibility, and the growing question of whether "correct" history is even a meaningful concept.

E) A document proving the divergence was engineered
   - Evidence that the POD was not an accident of history but a deliberate act -- someone or some organization changed the course of events on purpose. The document reveals who, how, and possibly why. Its existence threatens the foundation of the current world order.

F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** Alternate history burdens carry a distinctive philosophical weight: the question of whether there IS a "correct" timeline, and whether the protagonist has any right to impose one version of history over another. Build this ambiguity into the burden. The protagonist should increasingly doubt whether restoring the "true" timeline would even be just, given that millions of people exist and have lives only because of the divergence.

### Q1.2 -- The Fire

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS -- Question 2 of 10**

The Fire is your protagonist's internal power that threatens to consume them. In an alternate history, what burns inside them?

A) Forbidden knowledge of the true timeline
   - The protagonist knows how history "should" have gone -- through documents, visions, inherited knowledge, or scientific deduction. This knowledge gives them extraordinary insight into the weaknesses and fault lines of the current world order, but it also isolates them. No one believes them, or those who do want to use them. The more they learn about the true timeline, the less they can function in the present one.

B) Technology from the divergence that should not exist
   - The protagonist has access to technology that belongs to a path of development the divergence cut off -- a device, a formula, a technique that operates on principles this world's science never discovered. It works, powerfully, but it cannot be replicated, explained, or maintained within this timeline's knowledge base. Using it draws attention. Depending on it is dangerous.

C) The ability to perceive timeline echoes
   - The protagonist experiences flashes, visions, or intuitions of the "other" timeline -- the world that should have been. They see ghost images of buildings that were never built, hear echoes of conversations that never happened, feel the presence of people who were never born. This perception gives them uncanny insight but erodes their grip on the reality they actually inhabit. They are losing the ability to distinguish between what is and what should have been.

D) Connection to a suppressed historical truth
   - The protagonist carries knowledge -- familial, cultural, oral, or documentary -- of a history that the dominant power has erased. They are a living archive of what the regime, the empire, or the victors want forgotten. This knowledge gives them perspective that no one else has, but it also makes them a threat. The more they share it, the more dangerous their position becomes.

E) Identity crisis between who they are and who they "should" have been
   - The protagonist has become aware that in the "true" timeline, they (or their counterpart) were someone entirely different -- different profession, different allegiances, different relationships, different moral choices. They are haunted by the person they "should" have been, and the gap between that phantom self and their actual self is tearing them apart. The power is self-knowledge pushed to its destructive extreme.

F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** The alternate history Fire is distinctive because it is fundamentally about knowledge and perception rather than physical power. The protagonist's Fire burns not because they can do something extraordinary but because they KNOW something unbearable. The cost is psychological -- the erosion of certainty, identity, and the ability to live fully in the present.

### Q1.6 -- Antagonist's Truth

```
**CRUCIBLE THESIS -- Question 6 of 10**

What does the antagonist believe that is not entirely wrong?

A) "This timeline is the correct one and must be preserved"
   - The antagonist has built a life, a nation, or an empire in this timeline. The people they love, the institutions they serve, the world they know -- all exist because of the divergence. Restoring the "true" timeline would erase them. The antagonist is not wrong to fight for the survival of their world. They are wrong about the means, not the motivation.

B) "History bends toward the powerful -- I merely directed it"
   - The antagonist believes that the strong have always shaped history and that engineering the divergence was simply doing openly what every king, general, and president has done covertly. The powerful have always rewritten history to serve their interests. The antagonist is merely more honest about it. There is an uncomfortable truth here about how "real" history works.

C) "The divergence was necessary to prevent something worse"
   - The antagonist engineered or preserves the altered timeline because they believe (with evidence) that the original timeline led to catastrophe -- a world war, a plague, an extinction event, a technological horror. They changed history to save it. The protagonist must reckon with the possibility that the "true" timeline was worse than the altered one.

D) "There is no 'true' timeline -- only the one we make"
   - The antagonist's philosophy is that history has no inherent trajectory, no "correct" version. All timelines are equally real and equally valid. The concept of a "true" history is a comforting delusion. The antagonist is free to shape events because there is no moral baseline to violate. This is philosophically defensible and practically terrifying.

E) "Progress requires sacrifice -- the old world had to end"
   - The antagonist sees the divergence as an act of creative destruction. The old timeline was stagnant, unjust, or heading for decline. The new timeline, despite its costs, offers possibilities the old one never could. The millions who were never born, the nations that were never founded, the cultures that were erased -- these are the price of a better world. The antagonist is willing to pay that price. The protagonist must decide if they are.

F) Other (describe)
```

**Design note:** Alternate history antagonists are at their most compelling when they raise genuine philosophical questions about the nature of history itself. The best Dark Mirror figures force the protagonist to confront the possibility that there is no "right" version of events -- only versions with different winners and losers.

## Document 4: Constellation Strand Map

### Q4.4 -- Constellation Expansion

```
**CONSTELLATION -- Question 4 of 7**

What types of allies join in Movement III?

A) Historians and archivists who know the truth
   - Scholars, librarians, record-keepers, and researchers who have discovered the discrepancies between official history and the evidence. They have been working in secret, collecting proof, cross-referencing sources, and building an underground body of knowledge. They are meticulous, cautious, and terrified of exposure. What they lack in combat ability they make up for in the one resource that matters most in this genre: knowledge.

B) Resistance fighters against the altered regime
   - People who oppose the political order that the divergence created -- not because they know about the divergence, but because the regime is oppressive, unjust, or cruel in ways they can see and feel. They are practical, hardened, and experienced in clandestine operations. They may not care about restoring the "true" timeline; they simply want to overthrow this one.

C) Descendants of key historical figures
   - People whose ancestors played pivotal roles at the Point of Divergence -- the general who lost the battle, the scientist whose invention was suppressed, the leader who was assassinated or who survived. They carry family legends, inherited documents, and sometimes genetic markers that connect them to the turning point. Their personal history IS the world's history, and they know it.

D) Travelers and traders who have seen what other regions became
   - People who have moved through the altered world and witnessed how the divergence played out differently in different places. A trader who has seen the free cities beyond the empire's borders. A sailor who has visited the continent that developed along a completely different path. They carry firsthand knowledge of alternatives -- proof that the world did not have to be this way.

E) Scientists studying the divergence
   - Researchers who have detected anomalies -- physical, temporal, or informational -- that point to the divergence. They approach the altered timeline as a scientific problem rather than a political one. They want to understand the mechanism of the change, which may put them at odds with both the regime (which wants the change hidden) and the resistance (which wants to exploit it).

F) Other (describe)
```

## Document 9: World Forge

### Q9.1 -- The World's Wound

```
**WORLD FORGE -- Question 1 of 9**

What cosmic problem does the Quest address?

A) The divergence is destabilizing reality itself
   - The altered timeline is not sustainable. Temporal anomalies, paradoxes, or reality fractures are appearing -- evidence that the change was not meant to hold. The world is developing cracks. If nothing is done, the timeline may collapse entirely, taking everyone in it with them. The protagonist must either stabilize the altered timeline or find a way to transition to a sustainable one without destroying the people who live in this one.

B) An empire born from the divergence is expanding unchecked
   - The divergence created conditions for a power to rise that, in our timeline, never existed or never achieved dominance. This empire -- political, corporate, ideological, or theocratic -- is now extending its reach across continents or the globe. Its expansion is not simply military; it is cultural, economic, and philosophical. It is remaking the world in its image, and resistance is being crushed.

C) Technology from the divergence threatens global destruction
   - The altered path of technological development produced something that should not exist -- a weapon, an energy source, an industrial process that is poisoning the world. The technology is woven into the economy and power structure so deeply that removing it would collapse civilization. But allowing it to continue will destroy it. There is no clean solution.

D) The altered timeline is producing cascading failures
   - History has an inertia, and the divergence pushed it off a stable path. Consequences that took decades to develop are now cascading: ecological collapse because the wrong industries developed, political instability because the wrong borders were drawn, social fractures because the wrong ideologies prevailed. The world is not going to end in a single catastrophe. It is going to unravel, slowly and then all at once.

E) A secret organization is engineering further divergences
   - Someone -- a cabal, an institution, a lone genius -- has discovered how to create additional Points of Divergence and is using this power to reshape history according to their design. Each new divergence destabilizes the world further. The protagonist must find and stop them before the accumulated changes make any version of history unsustainable.

F) Other (describe)
```

### Q9.2 -- Power Source

```
**WORLD FORGE -- Question 2 of 9**

What is the source of power in this world?

A) Divergence-era technology
   - The altered path of development produced technologies that our world never achieved (or achieved differently). Power belongs to those who control this technology -- its production, its maintenance, its improvement. The technology itself shapes who can resist and who must comply.

B) Suppressed historical knowledge
   - Knowledge of what really happened -- the true history, the real divergence, the facts the regime has erased -- is the most dangerous and powerful resource in this world. Those who possess it can see the cracks in the official narrative, predict the regime's vulnerabilities, and mobilize others who sense that something is wrong.

C) Political connections in the altered power structure
   - In a world where different nations, families, or institutions hold power, access to the ruling class determines who prospers and who is marginalized. The altered power structure has its own aristocracy, its own old-boy networks, its own systems of patronage and favor. Navigating these systems -- or subverting them -- requires political skill.

D) Access to artifacts from the turning point
   - Physical objects that survive from the moment of divergence carry extraordinary significance. They are proof that the change occurred. They may also carry practical value -- a technology, a formula, a map, a key. Those who possess these artifacts hold leverage over both the regime and the resistance.

E) Understanding of how history "should" have gone
   - The rarest and most dangerous form of power: knowing the true timeline. This knowledge allows the protagonist to predict what the altered world will do wrong (because they know what it "should" have done) and to identify the fault lines where the altered history is weakest. It is strategic intelligence from another universe.

F) Other (describe)
```

### Q9.5 -- Key Locations

```
**WORLD FORGE -- Question 5 of 9**

What are the key locations in this world?

A) The site of the divergence point
   - The physical place where history changed. It may be a battlefield, a laboratory, a meeting hall, a crossroads, or a harbor. In the present, it may be a monument (if the winners celebrate the change), a ruin (if the change was destructive), or an unremarkable place (if the divergence has been forgotten or suppressed). Its significance is layered -- what happened there, what people believe happened there, and what actually changed.

B) The capital of the altered power
   - The seat of the empire, government, or institution that rose to dominance because of the divergence. This city is the physical embodiment of the altered timeline -- its architecture, its monuments, its street names, its cultural institutions all reflect the world the divergence made. It is impressive, functional, and built on a lie.

C) The underground archive or library
   - The place where the true history is preserved -- hidden, guarded, maintained by those who refuse to let it be erased. It may be a physical library, a network of safe houses, a digital archive, or a living oral tradition maintained by a community. It is the Sanctuary archetype in alternate history: the place where the truth survives.

D) The border between altered and less-affected regions
   - A geographic or political boundary beyond which the divergence's effects are weaker or different. On one side, the altered world. On the other, a place where remnants of the "true" timeline persist -- different customs, different technology, different social structures. The border is contested, patrolled, and charged with meaning. Crossing it changes the protagonist's understanding of what is possible.

E) The ruins of what existed before
   - Physical remnants of the world that the divergence erased -- buildings, infrastructure, monuments, cemeteries from the timeline that was overwritten. These ruins are evidence. They are also haunted, in the non-supernatural sense: they remind characters that real people lived in the "before," and that those lives were erased by the change.

F) The seat of resistance
   - The hidden or semi-hidden place where those who oppose the altered order gather, plan, and maintain their identity. It may be a tavern, a church, a farmhouse, an underground bunker, or a traveling market. Its location may shift. Its importance is constant: this is where the Constellation coalesces and where the protagonist finds people who believe what they believe.

G) Other (describe)
```

### Q9.8 -- History

```
**WORLD FORGE -- Question 8 of 9**

What is the deep history of this world?

In alternate history, this IS the core question. The world's history is not background -- it is the engine of the entire narrative. This question asks you to define three layers of the altered history.

A) The true history before the divergence
   - What actually happened before the POD? This is real history -- researched, accurate, and detailed. The stronger your grasp of what actually happened, the more powerful the divergence will be. Define the key events, figures, tensions, and trajectories of the pre-divergence world.

B) The divergence point and its immediate consequences
   - What changed, and what happened in the first generation afterward? Trace the immediate cascade: the battle won or lost, the technology invented or suppressed, the leader who lived or died. Show how the first-order effects reshaped the next decade. This is the foundation everything else is built on.

C) The long cascade -- what the world became
   - Follow the consequences across generations. How did borders shift? What nations rose or fell? What technologies developed or stalled? What cultural movements emerged or were prevented? What is the state of the world at the time of the story? This is the most demanding piece of world-building in alternate history, and it must be done rigorously.

D) What was preserved and what was lost
   - Not everything changes. Historical inertia is real. Some social structures, cultural patterns, and human behaviors persist regardless of the divergence. Define what survived the change and why. Equally, define what was irrevocably lost -- the inventions never made, the art never created, the freedoms never won. These absences haunt the story.

E) How the altered history is taught, remembered, and suppressed
   - Every society tells itself a story about its past. In your altered world, what story does the dominant power tell? What is celebrated, what is mourned, what is forbidden to discuss? How does official history differ from lived experience? How do marginalized groups preserve their own version of events? This layer determines how characters relate to their own world.

F) Other (describe)
```

## Additional Override Notes

### Questions That Use Default Options Unchanged

The following questions work as-is across genres and do not require alternate history overrides:

- **Q1.3 (Core Bond):** Romantic, familial, friendship, mentor, unlikely alliance -- these are universal.
- **Q1.4 (Dark Mirror Connection):** Same power/different choices, same origin/divergent paths -- these translate directly. In alternate history, the Dark Mirror often embodies a different response to the same historical knowledge.
- **Q1.5 (Forging):** What the protagonist must become is a character question, not a genre question.
- **Q1.7 (Surrender):** Power, certainty, vengeance, identity -- universal.
- **Q1.8 (Theme):** Thematic arguments are genre-agnostic. Alternate history themes often involve the nature of historical truth, the morality of power, and the question of whether history has a "correct" course.
- **Q2.1 (Burden Origin):** Inherited, discovered, thrust upon -- these mechanisms work in alternate history.
- **Q2.2 (Why Protagonist):** Ability, position, survival -- genre-neutral.
- **Q2.3 (Antagonist Stake):** Opposition motivations translate directly.
- **Q2.4 (Quest Escalation):** The default options (personal-to-world expansion) work well. In alternate history, personal discovery of the divergence escalating to world-level consequence is a natural pattern.
- **Q2.5 (Impossible Requirement):** Physical, sacrificial, temporal, knowledge-based -- all valid.
- **Q2.6 (Resolution):** Destroyed, transformed, passed on -- universal.
- **Q3.x (Fire Strand):** Manifestation, danger, cost, mastery -- these mechanics are genre-agnostic once Q1.2 establishes the alternate history Fire type.
- **Q4.1-4.3, Q4.5-4.6:** Faithful, sacrifice, betrayal, bond, fate -- character questions.
- **Q6.1-6.9:** Origin, divergence, want, offer, temptation, cost, defeat, end -- character questions. The Dark Mirror in alternate history often shares the protagonist's knowledge of the divergence but draws opposite conclusions.
- **Q9.3 (Limitations):** The default limitation options (resource scarcity, regulatory control, physical limits) translate well. In alternate history, the most powerful limitation is often informational -- the protagonist cannot prove what they know.
- **Q9.4 (Previous Wielders):** Succumbed, succeeded, destroyed -- applies to those who previously discovered the truth about the divergence.
- **Q9.6 (World Mirror):** How the world reflects the protagonist -- adaptable.
- **Q9.7 (Cultures):** Number of cultures is a structural choice, not genre-dependent.

### Adapting Default Options on the Fly

For questions using default options, the planner should still frame descriptions in alternate history language during the session. For example:

- Q2.1 option A ("Inherited -- death of mentor/relative") might be presented as: "Inherited when a historian or family elder dies, passing on documents, knowledge, or artifacts that reveal the truth about the divergence."
- Q4.1 option C ("Unlikely ally who becomes devoted") might be framed as: "A regime loyalist who discovers the truth about the divergence and switches sides, or a rival historian whose independent research confirms the protagonist's claims."
- Q6.1 option A ("Same origin, different choices") might be framed as: "Both discovered the truth about the divergence. One chose to expose it. The other chose to exploit it."

This contextual framing happens in the planner's presentation, not in the override file. The overrides above handle questions where the default options themselves do not fit the genre.
