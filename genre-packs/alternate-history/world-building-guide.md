# World-Building Guide: Alternate History

This guide provides structured thinking for building an alternate history world within the Crucible framework. Each section maps to elements of the World Forge planning document (Document 9) and connects to the three strands, Forge Points, and Mercy Engine.

Alternate history world-building is unique among genres because it begins with reality. You are not inventing a world from scratch. You are modifying one that already exists, and your modifications must be traceable, consistent, and honest. The discipline required is closer to engineering than to art -- though the best alternate history achieves both.

---

## The Point of Divergence (POD)

### How to Choose Your POD

The POD is the most consequential decision you will make. Everything else follows from it. Choose carefully.

**Criteria for a strong POD:**

1. **Historically plausible.** The divergence should feel like it COULD have happened. A different weather pattern at Waterloo. A musket ball that struck two inches to the left. A ship that arrived one day earlier. The most compelling PODs require only a small change in initial conditions.

2. **Consequentially rich.** The divergence should produce cascading effects across multiple domains -- political, technological, cultural, economic, military. A POD that only changes one thing is too narrow to sustain a novel.

3. **Emotionally resonant.** The divergence should matter to the reader. This does not mean it must involve a famous event -- but the reader should feel the weight of "this could have gone differently." The best PODs make the reader look at their own world and think, "We were this close to living in a different one."

4. **Research-accessible.** You must know the real history well enough to diverge from it credibly. Choose a period and event you can research thoroughly. If you cannot answer basic questions about the real history, you are not ready to alter it.

**Common POD categories:**

| Category | Examples | Strengths |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Military** | Battle outcomes, strategic decisions, weapon development | Clear, dramatic, easy to trace consequences |
| **Political** | Elections, assassinations, treaties, revolutions | Reshapes governance and ideology |
| **Technological** | Inventions earlier/later/differently, suppressed discoveries | Reshapes daily life and economy |
| **Epidemiological** | Plagues worse/milder/absent, different populations affected | Reshapes demographics and power |
| **Geographical** | Natural disasters, climate events, exploration routes | Reshapes borders and trade |
| **Cultural** | Religions spreading differently, philosophical movements | Reshapes values and social structures |
| **Individual** | Key figure lives/dies/makes different choice | Reshapes leadership and institutions |

### How to Trace Consequences

Start at the POD and work forward in layers.

**Layer 1: Immediate (0-10 years)**
- Who wins and who loses in the short term?
- What territory changes hands?
- What institutions survive and which collapse?
- What is the immediate emotional and cultural response?

**Layer 2: Medium-term (10-50 years)**
- How do the winners consolidate power?
- How do the losers adapt, resist, or assimilate?
- What new institutions, alliances, and rivalries emerge?
- How does the change affect the next generation -- people who grow up in the altered world?

**Layer 3: Long-term (50-200 years)**
- How has the political map settled?
- What technologies developed along the altered path?
- How has culture, religion, and philosophy responded to the different history?
- What are the new "normal" assumptions about how the world works?

**Layer 4: Deep consequences (200+ years)**
- What does the world look like when no one alive remembers the alternative?
- How is the divergence (if known) taught, mythologized, or suppressed?
- What second- and third-order effects have compounded into features of daily life?

### The Butterfly Effect vs. Historical Inertia

This is the central tension in alternate history world-building. The butterfly effect says small changes can produce enormous consequences. Historical inertia says large systems resist change.

Both are true. The skill is knowing which applies where.

**Things that change easily:**
- The outcome of a single battle or election
- The career of a specific individual
- The timing of a specific invention or discovery
- Short-term political alliances

**Things that resist change:**
- Fundamental economic patterns (trade follows geography)
- Demographic trends (populations grow or decline based on resources and disease, not policy)
- Deep cultural patterns (religions evolve slowly; family structures persist across regimes)
- Geographical constraints (rivers still flow the same way; mountain passes still control trade routes)
- Human nature (ambition, fear, love, greed, curiosity persist regardless of timeline)

**The practical rule:** For each consequence you trace, ask: "Is this change driven by the POD's specific effects, or am I just making things different because I can?" If the latter, apply inertia. If the former, trace the chain of causation and make sure each link holds.

## Extrapolation Methodology

### First-Order Effects

These are the direct, immediate consequences of the POD. They require minimal extrapolation -- they are what obviously happens next.

**Example POD:** The Spanish Armada succeeds in 1588.
**First-order effects:**
- Spain invades England. The English navy is broken.
- Elizabeth I is deposed or killed.
- England becomes Catholic (or descends into religious civil war).
- English colonial ventures in the New World stall or stop.

### Second-Order Effects

These are the consequences of the first-order effects. They require you to think about systems rather than events.

**Continuing the example:**
- Without English colonization, the eastern seaboard of North America develops differently -- French, Dutch, and Spanish settlement patterns dominate.
- The English language does not become a global lingua franca. Spanish and French compete for dominance.
- The Protestant Reformation is weakened across Europe without England as a Protestant power.
- The balance of naval power in the 17th century shifts permanently toward Spain.

### What Changes and What Stays the Same

For every domain of life, explicitly decide what the POD changed and what it did not.

| Domain | Changed | Stayed the Same |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **Politics** | Which nations hold power | The drive for power itself |
| **Technology** | Which inventions emerged and when | The underlying science (physics does not change) |
| **Culture** | Which art, literature, music dominated | The human need for creative expression |
| **Religion** | Which faiths spread and where | The human need for meaning and community |
| **Economics** | Which trade routes, which industries | The laws of supply and demand |
| **Social structure** | Which classes hold power | The existence of class structures |
| **Warfare** | Which weapons, which tactics | The willingness to use violence for power |

This table is a starting point. Fill it in for your specific POD. The "Stayed the Same" column is as important as the "Changed" column -- it keeps your world grounded and prevents it from feeling arbitrarily different.

## Technology Paths

### How Different Outcomes Lead to Different Tech Trees

Technology does not develop in a vacuum. It develops because specific economic, military, and social pressures create demand, and specific resource availability and knowledge bases make supply possible. Change those pressures and resources, and the technology path changes.

**Key questions:**
- What problems does this society need to solve? (Demand drives invention.)
- What resources are available? (Material constraints shape solutions.)
- What knowledge base exists? (You cannot invent the transistor without understanding semiconductors.)
- Who funds research? (Military, commercial, academic, religious patrons produce different technologies.)
- What cultural attitudes affect development? (A society that fears automation develops differently from one that embraces it.)

### What Accelerates

Technologies accelerate when the altered history creates stronger demand or earlier knowledge:
- A longer Roman Empire might accelerate engineering, road-building, and hydraulics.
- A successful Chinese age of exploration might accelerate maritime technology and cartography.
- A world war that occurs 50 years earlier might accelerate weapons development on a different tech base.

### What Stalls

Technologies stall when the altered history removes demand, resources, or precursor knowledge:
- Without the industrial revolution, precision manufacturing develops slowly or not at all.
- Without the Cold War, space technology may develop decades later.
- Without the printing press, literacy remains limited and information technology stalls.
- Without the specific economic pressures that drove the development of computing, digital technology may never emerge in a recognizable form.

### Technology Cascades

Remember that technologies enable other technologies. A stall in one area cascades:
- No precision manufacturing means no reliable firearms, no interchangeable parts, no assembly lines.
- No assembly lines means no mass production, no consumer economy, no middle class as we know it.
- No middle class means different political structures, different education systems, different cultural production.

Trace these cascades honestly. They are the backbone of believable alternate history world-building.

## Geopolitics

### How Borders, Alliances, and Empires Shift

Political geography is one of the most visibly affected domains after a POD. Different wars, treaties, and power dynamics produce different maps.

**Guidelines for redrawing the map:**

1. **Follow geography.** Rivers, mountain ranges, coastlines, and fertile regions shape borders in every timeline. A different POD changes WHO controls a river valley, but the river valley is still strategically important.

2. **Follow resources.** Oil fields, mineral deposits, arable land, and trade chokepoints determine which regions are worth fighting over. The resources do not move; only the powers competing for them change.

3. **Follow demographics.** Population centers persist because they are located where people can live -- near water, on trade routes, in fertile areas. A different POD changes who lives there and under what government, but the city likely still exists.

4. **Follow cultural fault lines.** Ethnic, linguistic, and religious boundaries are among the most persistent features of human geography. Different political outcomes may draw borders that cross these lines differently, but the lines themselves persist.

### Cascade Effects on Other Nations

A divergence that changes the fate of one nation cascades across its neighbors and trading partners:

- A stronger Spain weakens England, France, and the Netherlands in different ways.
- A surviving Ottoman Empire changes the entire trajectory of the Middle East, Eastern Europe, and North Africa.
- A unified China that never experiences the Century of Humiliation becomes a different kind of global power.

For each major nation in your story's scope, ask: "How did the POD change this nation's trajectory?" Even nations far from the POD's geographic center are affected through trade, migration, cultural exchange, and the shifting balance of power.

## Culture and Society

### How Art, Religion, and Philosophy Change -- Or Do Not

Cultural change after a POD is subtler and slower than political change. Cultures are resilient systems with deep roots.

**Art and literature:**
- Art reflects the society that produces it. Different political structures, different dominant religions, and different social anxieties produce different artistic movements.
- But art also serves universal human needs. People still tell stories, make music, paint, and dance. The forms change; the impulse does not.
- Consider what was never created. In a world without the French Revolution, Romanticism develops differently or not at all. No Romanticism means different music, literature, and philosophy for the next two centuries.

**Religion:**
- Religions spread along trade routes and through conquest. A different military and commercial history means different religious geography.
- Theological development responds to political and social context. A Christianity that never experienced the Reformation is a different faith than one that did.
- Religious minorities in different positions of power produce different social dynamics. A world where Judaism or Islam is dominant in Europe reshapes everything from architecture to law to cuisine.

**Philosophy and ideology:**
- Political ideologies emerge from specific historical conditions. Liberalism, socialism, fascism, and democracy as we know them are products of specific European circumstances. Change those circumstances, and different ideologies emerge.
- But the underlying human questions persist: Who should rule? What is justice? What do we owe each other? Different ideologies are different answers to the same questions.

**Language:**
- Languages spread with empires and trade. Different empires mean different lingua francas.
- Vocabulary reflects technology and culture. A world without automobiles has no word for "highway." A world without democracy may lack the word "vote" in its modern sense.
- Dialect and accent carry social meaning. Which dialect carries prestige depends on who holds power.

## Historical Figures

### How to Handle Real People in Altered Circumstances

Using real historical figures in alternate history is powerful but demands care.

**Core principle:** A historical figure's fundamental character traits should survive the divergence. Their circumstances, opportunities, and choices should change.

- **Abraham Lincoln** in a world without the Civil War might be a successful railroad lawyer, a state governor, or a minor political figure. He is still intelligent, empathetic, rhetorically gifted, and ambitious. He just never had the crucible (lowercase) that forged him into the Lincoln we know.
- **Napoleon Bonaparte** in a world where France never had a revolution might be a brilliant artillery officer who retired as a colonel. His strategic genius, his ambition, his charisma -- these are personal traits. His empire was a product of specific historical conditions.
- **Queen Victoria** in a world where Britain lost to the Armada might never exist. The butterfly effect on genealogy is real. If you need a recognizable figure, use someone born close to the POD. The further from the POD, the less likely specific individuals are to exist.

**The genealogical cutoff:**
- Figures born BEFORE the POD can appear. Their early life is the same; their later life diverges.
- Figures born WITHIN one generation after the POD might exist but might not, depending on how much the divergence affected their parents' circumstances.
- Figures born MORE than one generation after the POD almost certainly do not exist as we know them. Different circumstances mean different marriages, different births, different people.

**Maintaining believable characterization:**
- Research the real person thoroughly. Read their letters, speeches, and contemporaries' accounts.
- Identify their core traits versus their historically contingent traits. Separate who they WERE from what they DID.
- Let the altered circumstances reveal different aspects of their character. A Lincoln who never faced the moral crucible of slavery might be complacent. A Napoleon who never had an army might channel his ambition into commerce or science.
- Do not use historical figures as props. If they appear, they must be fully realized characters with their own arcs, not cameos.

## Economy

### How Trade Routes, Resources, and Industry Change

Economic systems are deeply affected by political and technological changes, but they also follow their own logic.

**Trade routes:**
- Trade follows geography, demand, and political stability. Different empires control different routes, but the routes themselves -- through mountain passes, along rivers, across oceans -- persist.
- A dominant Mediterranean power controls different trade than a dominant Atlantic power. A Chinese-dominated Pacific trade network looks very different from a European-dominated one.
- Monopolies and trade restrictions shape economies. Who controls the spice trade, the silk road, the oil supply? Different answers produce different worlds.

**Resource extraction:**
- The resources are where they are. Oil is in the Middle East and Texas regardless of who controls those regions. Gold is in California and South Africa. The question is who controls extraction and who profits.
- Different technological paths mean different resources matter. A world that never developed internal combustion does not need oil the same way. A world that developed solar power early might fight over lithium instead.

**Industrial development:**
- Industrialization requires preconditions: available capital, available labor, available resources, available knowledge, and market demand. Change any of these, and industrialization happens differently or not at all.
- A world where the Industrial Revolution began in China rather than England produces a fundamentally different global economy.
- Consider what industries NEVER developed. No automobile industry means no suburbs, no highway system, no oil economy, no Middle East geopolitics as we know them.

## Military and Warfare

### How Different Wars and Outcomes Reshape Military Doctrine

Military development is one of the most sensitive areas to historical change. Armies learn from their last war, and different last wars produce different armies.

**Doctrine follows experience:**
- An army that won its last war with cavalry charges does not develop infantry tactics the same way.
- A navy that never lost to submarines does not develop anti-submarine warfare.
- A nation that never experienced aerial bombing does not develop air defense or strategic bombing doctrine.

**Technology follows demand:**
- Weapons development responds to military needs. Different wars create different needs.
- Defensive advantages last until offensive technology catches up. In your alternate world, this balance may be different.
- Consider what was never invented. A world without the trench warfare of WWI might never develop tanks. A world without the Pacific island-hopping campaign might never develop amphibious assault doctrine.

**The human cost:**
- Different wars mean different veteran populations, different collective traumas, different cultural attitudes toward violence and service.
- The Crucible's Hardening phase maps powerfully to soldiers in an alternate war -- fighting for a cause that exists only because history went differently.
- The Mercy Engine is especially potent in military contexts. Showing mercy to an enemy soldier whose entire nation exists because of the divergence raises the question: is this person's life less real because their world "should not" exist?

## Daily Life

### How Ordinary People Experience the Altered World

This is where alternate history becomes immersive. The grand sweep of altered geopolitics means nothing if the reader cannot feel what it is like to live in this world day to day.

**Food:**
- Cuisines depend on available ingredients, trade routes, cultural exchange, and immigration patterns. Different empires produce different culinary fusions.
- A world without the Columbian Exchange (if the Americas were never colonized by Europeans) means no tomatoes in Italy, no potatoes in Ireland, no chilies in India. This single change reshapes global cuisine entirely.

**Fashion:**
- Clothing reflects available materials, manufacturing capability, cultural values, and climate. Different technological paths produce different textiles.
- Fashion also reflects power. The dominant culture's aesthetic spreads through trade and imitation. Different dominant cultures mean different global aesthetics.

**Transportation:**
- Without specific technological developments, transportation looks different. A world without railroads (because the steam engine developed later or differently) is a world where travel is slower, trade is more local, and cities are denser.
- Different transportation changes the pace of life, the size of nations, and the experience of distance.

**Communication:**
- The history of communication technology is a cascade: printing press, telegraph, telephone, radio, television, internet. Remove or delay any link in this chain, and everything after it changes.
- A world without the telegraph is a world where news travels at the speed of a horse or a ship. Political decisions are made with weeks-old information. Wars are fought by commanders who cannot communicate in real time.

**Housing and architecture:**
- Buildings reflect available materials, construction technology, cultural values, climate, and wealth distribution. Different technological and political histories produce different built environments.
- A world dominated by a different architectural tradition looks and feels different at the most basic level -- the shapes of doorways, the pitch of roofs, the materials of walls.

**Medicine:**
- Medical development follows scientific knowledge, institutional support, and cultural attitudes. A world without germ theory (if Pasteur's work was suppressed or never done) is a world where surgery is still deadly, epidemics are unexplained, and life expectancy is shorter.
- Different dominant medical traditions (Chinese, Ayurvedic, Galenic) produce different healthcare systems and different attitudes toward the body.

## Research Methodology

### How to Research the Real History You Are Diverging From

You cannot credibly alter what you do not understand. Research is not optional in alternate history -- it is the foundation.

**Primary sources:**
- Read documents from the era: letters, diaries, government records, newspapers, speeches, legal codes. These give you the texture of how people thought, spoke, and lived.
- Primary sources reveal details that secondary sources summarize away. The specific words a general used in an order. The price of bread in a specific city in a specific year. The complaints of a farmer about the weather.

**Secondary sources:**
- Read academic histories of the period, especially those that examine causation -- WHY things happened, not just WHAT happened.
- Look for historians who discuss contingency -- moments where history could have gone differently. These are your POD candidates.
- Read military history for battles, economic history for trade and industry, social history for daily life, and cultural history for art, religion, and ideas.

**Counterfactual literature:**
- Read existing alternate history novels set in your period. Not to copy, but to see what has been done and what has been overlooked.
- Read academic counterfactual essays (like those in "Virtual History" edited by Niall Ferguson). Historians have already done some of the extrapolation work for popular PODs.

**Period accuracy checklist:**
- [ ] Do I know what ordinary people ate, wore, and lived in?
- [ ] Do I know what technology existed and what did not?
- [ ] Do I know how people traveled and communicated?
- [ ] Do I know the political structure and who held power?
- [ ] Do I know the social hierarchies -- class, gender, race, religion?
- [ ] Do I know the economic system -- what was traded, what was scarce, what was valuable?
- [ ] Do I know how this society handled crime, medicine, education, and religion?
- [ ] Do I know the specific event I am diverging from in enough detail to change it credibly?

## Mapping the World Forge to Alternate History World-Building

The World Forge planning document (Document 9) asks nine questions. Here is how each maps to alternate history specifics.

### Q9.1 -- The World's Wound

In alternate history, **the World's Wound IS the divergence and its consequences.** This is not a metaphor. The POD created the conditions that produced the story's central conflict. The wound is the altered world itself -- or, more precisely, the specific ways the altered world is failing, oppressing, or destroying.

When answering Q9.1, connect the cosmic problem directly to the POD:
- If the POD created an empire, the Wound is the empire's expansion and oppression.
- If the POD changed technology, the Wound is the technology's destructive potential.
- If the POD prevented a liberation, the Wound is the continued subjugation.

The reader should be able to trace a clear line from POD to Wound.

### Q9.2 -- Power Source

In alternate history, power is often informational rather than physical. The most dangerous thing you can possess is knowledge -- of the true history, of the divergence's mechanism, of the regime's vulnerabilities.

Physical power sources (technology, military force, economic control) are also valid, but they should be connected to the divergence. The technology that grants power exists BECAUSE of the altered history. The military that enforces order was built on the altered timeline's foundations.

### Q9.3 -- Limitations

Alternate history limitations are often informational and social rather than physical:
- The protagonist cannot PROVE what they know. Evidence has been destroyed or discredited.
- The protagonist cannot act openly. The regime is watching.
- The protagonist cannot trust fully. In a world built on a lie, who else is living in the truth?
- The protagonist cannot undo the divergence without consequences. Real people's lives depend on the altered timeline.

These limitations create tension that is specific to the genre and cannot be resolved by physical force alone.

### Q9.4 -- Previous Wielders

In alternate history, "previous wielders" are those who previously discovered the truth about the divergence:
- Historians who got too close and were silenced.
- Resistance leaders who tried to act on the knowledge and were crushed.
- Scientists who detected anomalies and were discredited.
- Family members who passed down forbidden knowledge before dying.

Their fates foreshadow the protagonist's risk and establish the genre's stakes: knowing the truth is dangerous.

### Q9.5 -- Key Locations

Alternate history locations serve the same archetypal functions as in any Crucible story, but they carry additional layers of meaning:

| Archetype | Alternate History Equivalent | Significance |
|-----------|------------------------------|-------------|
| Ordinary World | The protagonist's daily life in the altered world | Shows the texture of the changed world before the Quest begins |
| Threshold | The moment/place where the protagonist encounters evidence of the divergence | The point of no return -- once you know, you cannot un-know |
| Sanctuary | The underground archive, the resistance safe house | Where the truth is preserved and the Constellation gathers |
| Corrupted Institution | The seat of the altered power, the propaganda ministry | The divergence made manifest in architecture and authority |
| Gathering Place | Where resistance coalesces | The Constellation's physical home |
| Dark Heart | The site of the divergence itself, or the place where the truth is most fiercely guarded | Where the protagonist confronts the origin of the altered world |

### Q9.6 -- World Mirror

In alternate history, the world mirrors the protagonist through the tension between what IS and what SHOULD BE:
- Early in the story, the world seems stable and the protagonist is the one who is "wrong." The mirror shows a functional society and a malfunctioning individual.
- As the protagonist uncovers the truth, the world's cracks become visible. The mirror now shows a broken world and a protagonist who is becoming whole through knowledge.
- In the Hardening phase, the protagonist may begin to doubt whether the "true" timeline was actually better. The mirror clouds. Neither world is clearly right.

### Q9.7 -- Cultures

In alternate history, cultures are specifically shaped by the divergence:
- The dominant culture is the culture of whoever won because of the POD.
- Suppressed cultures are those who lost because of the POD.
- Hybrid cultures emerge in border regions and trade zones.
- Resistance cultures preserve pre-divergence values and knowledge.

Multiple cultures create opportunities for the Constellation strand -- allies from different cultural backgrounds who experience the altered world differently.

### Q9.8 -- History

**This IS the central world-building element in alternate history.** In every other genre, history is background. In alternate history, history is the story. The question overrides for Q9.8 in this pack reflect this by asking the author to define five layers: the true pre-divergence history, the POD and its immediate consequences, the long cascade, what was preserved and lost, and how history is taught and suppressed.

Do not treat Q9.8 as a background question to be answered quickly. This is where the real work of alternate history world-building happens. Spend more time here than on any other World Forge question.

## World-Building Checklist

Before moving from planning to outlining, confirm:

- [ ] Point of Divergence defined with specific event, date, and mechanism
- [ ] First-order consequences traced (0-10 years)
- [ ] Second-order consequences traced (10-50 years)
- [ ] Third-order consequences traced (50-200+ years) if story is set that far from POD
- [ ] Technology path defined (what exists, what does not, what is different)
- [ ] Political map drawn (who controls what territory and why)
- [ ] Cultural consequences explored (art, religion, language, philosophy)
- [ ] Historical figures handled appropriately (genealogical cutoff respected)
- [ ] Economic system defined (trade routes, resources, currency, industry)
- [ ] Military doctrine and capability defined (if relevant to story)
- [ ] Daily life details established (food, fashion, transportation, communication, housing)
- [ ] The "what stayed the same" column filled in (historical inertia acknowledged)
- [ ] The World's Wound connected directly to the POD
- [ ] Key locations mapped to Crucible archetypes with alternate history layering
- [ ] History as taught in the altered world defined (official narrative, suppressed truths, contested memories)
- [ ] Research sources documented (for reference during writing)
- [ ] The three Crucible strands connected to the divergence (Quest involves the divergence, Fire involves knowledge or perception of the divergence, Constellation involves trust in a world built on altered foundations)
