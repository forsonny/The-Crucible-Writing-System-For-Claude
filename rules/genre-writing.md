# Genre Writing Rules

These base rules apply to ALL genres. Genre-specific rules from the selected genre pack are loaded alongside these.

## Universal Prose Guidelines

- Balance action with introspection
- Use sensory details for immersion
- Show culture through character behavior
- Weave world-building into action, not info-dumps
- Speech patterns should reflect character background
- Formal/informal register should match setting
- Accents and dialects used sparingly and consistently

## Speculative System Rules

Every genre has a "speculative system" — the element that makes the story's world different from ours. In fantasy it's magic; in sci-fi it's technology; in thrillers it's the conspiracy/power structure; in alternate history it's the divergence point.

### Sanderson's Laws (Adapted for All Genres)

**First Law:** A speculative system (magic, technology, power structure) can solve problems proportionally to how well the reader understands its rules.

**Second Law:** Limitations are more interesting than capabilities. Define what the system CAN'T do.

**Third Law:** Expand what you already have before adding something new.

### Cost and Consequence
- All power (magical, technological, political, personal) should have a cost
- Costs should be meaningful
- Consequences should be consistent
- No unlimited power without unlimited consequence

## Character Requirements (All Genres)

### Protagonist
- Clear motivation that drives the plot
- Internal conflict that creates growth
- Agency in their own story
- Flaws that create obstacles
- Growth arc that ties to theme

### Supporting Cast
- Each character serves narrative purpose
- Distinct voices and personalities
- Own goals beyond helping protagonist
- Relationships that evolve

### Antagonist Depth
- Believes they are justified
- Has understandable motivation
- Poses genuine threat
- Thematically mirrors protagonist

## Genre Pack Loading

The selected genre pack adds genre-specific rules on top of these base rules. See `genre-packs/<genre>/genre-rules.md` for the active genre's conventions.
